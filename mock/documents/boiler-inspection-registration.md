## 1. Purpose and summary

### 1.1 Purpose

This report records the annual external in-service inspection of the Fulton FB-030
vertical gas fired steam boiler installed at Shop 4, 118 Hargreaves Street, Bendigo,
carried out on 24 June 2026 in accordance with AS 3788 *Pressure equipment — In-service
inspection*, and records the particulars of the Victorian plant registration held for
that item.

It also records the safety valve test, the safety device function tests, the water
treatment review, the operator competency and log review, and the observations and
defects arising, with the action required and the date by which each must be completed.

### 1.2 Summary of outcome

The boiler was found to be in sound condition and fit for continued service at its
registered maximum allowable working pressure of 690 kPa, subject to the rectification of
the two defects listed at Section 9 within the times stated there.

No condition was found that required the boiler to be taken out of service. No condition
was found that required a reduction in the maximum allowable working pressure. The safety
valve lifted, passed and reseated within tolerance. The water level controls and the low
water cut-out operated correctly on test. Boiler water chemistry was within limits, with a
rising total dissolved solids trend noted for attention.

Two matters require action:

- **Item 1** — the lower gauge glass gland is weeping and must be repacked. **Category 3,
  30 days, by 24 July 2026.**
- **Item 2** — the bottom blowdown valve packing is worn and must be replaced. **Category
  4, at the next shutdown and in any event by 30 November 2026.**

Twelve further observations are recorded at Section 9. None of them affects fitness for
service. One of them — the three gaps in the daily operating log during May 2026 — is a
management system matter that the operator must close out, and is discussed at Section 11.

## 4. Scope and standards applied

### 4.1 Scope of this inspection

This was an **external in-service inspection** conducted with the boiler cold at the
commencement of the inspection, then fired and brought to working pressure for the
functional testing. It comprised:

- visual examination of all accessible external surfaces of the pressure envelope, the
  casing having been opened at the front and the rear access panels removed;
- ultrasonic thickness measurement at 14 locations on the shell, heads and furnace;
- examination of all fittings, mountings, connections and pressure retaining components
  accessible without dismantling;
- examination of the safety valve, and a lift test on steam under boiler pressure;
- function testing of the water level controls, the low water cut-out, the high pressure
  limit, the flame safeguard and the gas train safety devices;
- examination of the feedwater system, the hotwell, the softener and the chemical dosing
  arrangement;
- examination of the blowdown arrangement and its discharge;
- examination of the gas train and the flue system;
- combustion analysis at high fire and at low fire;
- sampling and testing of boiler water, feedwater and condensate;
- review of the operating logs, the water treatment records, the maintenance records and
  the operator competency records; and
- review of the close-out of items raised in the previous report.

### 4.2 What this inspection did not cover

This was not an internal inspection. The pressure envelope was not entered and the
waterside and fireside internal surfaces were not directly examined; the last internal
examination was on 18 June 2024 and the next is due in June 2028. No hydrostatic test was
performed; the next is due in 2029. The furnace refractory was viewed through the burner
opening only. Buried, insulated or otherwise inaccessible surfaces were not examined
except where thickness readings were taken through prepared windows. The steam
reticulation beyond the crown valve and the first isolating valve, and the steam-using
equipment, are outside the scope of this report.

Nothing in this report is a statement about the condition of the electrical installation
beyond the safety devices tested, or about the structural adequacy of the building.

## 5. Inspection methodology

### 5.1 Sequence followed on the day

The inspection was performed over five and a half hours on 24 June 2026, commencing at
07:15 with the boiler cold and off line from the previous evening.

1. **Documentation review, 07:15 to 08:00.** Registration, previous report, logs, water
   treatment reports, maintenance records and operator licences reviewed with the
   responsible officer and the plant operator before any physical work commenced.
2. **Isolation and permit, 08:00 to 08:15.** The boiler was confirmed off line, cold, and
   at atmospheric pressure. The gas was isolated at the manual shut-off cock and locked.
   The electrical supply was isolated and tagged. The crown valve was closed. A permit was
   raised and countersigned by the operator.
3. **External examination cold, 08:15 to 09:45.** Casing panels removed at the front and
   rear. Visual examination of the shell, heads, furnace throat, nozzles, attachments,
   supports and the underside of the boiler. Examination of all mountings and fittings.
   Examination of the gauge glass, the level control chamber, the blowdown arrangement,
   the feedwater connection and the safety valve installation.
4. **Ultrasonic thickness testing, 09:45 to 10:45.** Fourteen locations, prepared by wire
   brushing to bare metal where insulation and paint allowed, with couplant, three readings
   per location and the lowest recorded. Locations marked and recorded so they can be
   repeated at the next inspection. See Appendix A.
5. **Feedwater, softener and chemical dosing examination, 10:45 to 11:15.** Hotwell,
   feedwater pumps, non-return and isolating valves, softener resin bed and brine
   saturator, chemical dosing pump and pot.
6. **Water sampling and testing, 11:15 to 11:45.** Boiler water drawn from the sample cock
   after a purge and cooled in a stainless sample cooler; feedwater from the hotwell
   outlet; condensate from the return line. Tested on site. See Section 10.
7. **Return to service and warm-up, 11:45 to 12:45.** Permit closed, isolations removed,
   gas restored, boiler fired and brought up slowly to working pressure with the operator
   at the panel.
8. **Functional testing at pressure, 12:45 to 13:45.** Gauge glass blowdown, level control
   tests, low water cut-out by controlled evaporation, high pressure limit, flame failure,
   gas pressure switches, purge timing, and the safety valve lift test. See Sections 6, 7,
   8 and Appendix B.
9. **Combustion analysis, 13:45 to 14:10.** At high fire after 10 minutes of stable firing,
   and at low fire.
10. **Close-out with the operator and the responsible officer, 14:10 to 14:45.** Findings
    walked through at the boiler, defects and required dates explained, photographs taken.

### 5.2 Acceptance criteria applied

- **Wall thickness.** A reading is acceptable where it is at or above the minimum required
  thickness of 8.6 mm for the shell and 10.8 mm for the furnace. A reading within 10% of
  the minimum required thickness is reportable and triggers a shortened re-measurement
  interval. The rate of loss is assessed against the 2018 and 2024 datum readings.
- **Surface defects.** Any crack, lamination, gouge, arc strike or bulge in a pressure part
  is a Category 1 finding. Isolated pitting is assessed against the remaining thickness at
  the pit base.
- **Leakage.** Any leakage of steam or water from a pressure part or a pressure retaining
  joint is reportable. Leakage from a packed gland is assessed on its rate and its
  location; a weep that can be contained by repacking is Category 3, an active leak that
  cannot be isolated is Category 1.
- **Safety valve.** Popping pressure within +3% / -0% of set pressure; reseat within 10% of
  set pressure; no chatter; no leakage after reseat.
- **Safety devices.** Each device must operate at its set point and must produce the
  required response — alarm, lockout, or shutdown with manual reset as applicable. Flame
  failure lockout within 1 second of flame loss.
- **Water chemistry.** Assessed against the limits at Section 10, which are the water
  treatment supplier's limits for this boiler type and duty and are consistent with
  AS 2593 guidance.

## 6. Detailed findings — pressure parts, fittings and systems

### 6.1 Shell, heads and external pressure envelope

The shell was examined externally over its full accessible height, front, rear and both
sides, with the casing panels removed and the insulation lifted at the prepared thickness
test windows.

The external surface is sound. The paint system is intact over the great majority of the
shell. There is light surface oxidation at the lower shell adjacent the blowdown
connection, consistent with historical minor weeping from the blowdown valve gland and
consistent with observation item 2. The oxidation is superficial; a wire brush and probe
found sound metal beneath and no pitting of consequence. There is no bulging, no
distortion, no evidence of overheating, no arc strike, no gouge and no crack.

The top head and the crown are sound and dry. The bottom head and the boiler underside
were examined with a mirror and a torch and are dry, with no accumulation of debris or
water beneath the boiler.

Ultrasonic thickness readings are recorded at Appendix A. The lowest shell reading was
10.1 mm against a nominal 10.0 mm and a minimum required 8.6 mm. The lowest furnace
reading was 12.2 mm against a minimum required 10.8 mm. Comparison against the 2024 datum
shows no measurable loss within the resolution of the instrument. There is no wastage
requiring action, and no basis on wastage grounds to shorten any interval.

### 6.2 Furnace, tubesheet and the 2021 weld repair

The furnace throat and the furnace-to-tubesheet attachment were examined visually through
the burner opening with the burner head withdrawn, and by magnetic particle examination of
the accessible portion of the 2021 repair weld and its heat affected zone.

The repair weld is sound. No linear indication, no crack, no undercut of consequence and
no evidence of service-induced cracking were found. The weld cap profile is smooth and
blends well into the parent metal. The magnetic particle examination covered 340 mm of the
accessible weld length; the balance is not accessible without entry and will be examined
at the 2028 internal inspection.

The refractory at the furnace throat is intact, with a hairline surface craze pattern that
is normal for the material and the service and is not a defect. The furnace internal
surface visible through the opening is clean and carries a light even oxide with no
scaling, no soot loading and no evidence of flame impingement.

### 6.7 Gauge glass

A single reflex gauge glass is fitted in a bronze frame with upper and lower fittings,
each with an integral isolating cock and a ball check, and with a drain cock at the lower
fitting. The normal water level is marked on the frame.

The glass itself is clean, clear, undamaged, free of chipping and free of the vertical
etching that indicates an aged glass, and the water line was clearly visible from the
operating position at all times during the inspection.

The gauge glass was blown down under the inspector's observation. The water returned
briskly and settled within 3 seconds, which indicates that both connections are clear.
The upper and lower cocks operate freely and seal.

**The lower gland is weeping.** A steady bead of moisture forms at the lower packing nut
and runs down the fitting and onto the shell below. The weep is small — a drop forming
roughly every 40 seconds at working pressure — and it is contained, and it does not
prevent the gauge from performing its function. It is nevertheless a leak from a pressure
retaining joint and it must be corrected. The gland nut has already been taken up to the
end of its available travel, so tightening further will not help; the packing is spent and
must be renewed. This is **item 1** at Section 9 and is required within 30 days.

The staining on the shell below the gauge glass is the mineral residue from this weep. It
was cleaned back during the inspection so that the staining seen at the next inspection
will be new staining and not old.

No spare gauge glass, gasket set or packing is held on site. That is recorded as
observation 11.

### 9.2 Items requiring action

**Item 1 — Gauge glass lower gland weeping. Category 3. Rectify by 24 July 2026.**

*Finding.* The lower gauge glass fitting is weeping steadily at the packing gland,
producing a drop approximately every 40 seconds at working pressure, running down the
fitting and staining the shell below. The gland nut has been taken up to the end of its
available travel and further tightening will not stop the weep; the packing is spent.

*Significance.* This is a leak from a pressure retaining joint on a fitting that the
operator relies on to read the water level. It is small and contained today. Left alone,
the packing will continue to degrade, the leak rate will rise, the wetting will accelerate
external corrosion of the fitting and the shell beneath it, and eventually the fitting may
be difficult to isolate without shutting the boiler down at short notice. Steam at 620 kPa
from a failed gland is a burn hazard to anyone standing at the gauge glass, which is
exactly where the operator stands each morning.

*Required action.* Isolate, depressurise and repack the lower gauge glass gland with the
correct grade of packing. While the fitting is apart, renew the glass and both gaskets,
prove the connection clear, and check the ball check operates. On return to service, blow
the gauge down, confirm the water returns and settles within 5 seconds, and confirm the
gland is dry at working pressure.

*Required by.* **24 July 2026** — 30 days from the date of inspection.

*Evidence required.* The repair must be recorded in the plant maintenance record with the
date, the person who carried out the work, and the parts used. That record will be sighted
at the next inspection. Photographic evidence of the dry gland at working pressure emailed
to this office will close the item out earlier.

*Competency.* This work may be carried out by a competent maintenance person or by the
boiler service contractor. It is not work that requires a pressure equipment inspector.

---

**Item 2 — Bottom blowdown valve packing worn. Category 4. Rectify at the next shutdown
and in any event by 30 November 2026.**

*Finding.* The slow opening screw-down blowdown valve shows wear in the gland packing. The
gland nut is near the end of its travel, and a trace of moisture appears at the gland
after the valve is operated, although the valve seals on its seat and is dry between
operations. The light surface oxidation on the lower shell adjacent this connection,
noted at 6.1, is the historical residue of this condition.

*Significance.* The blowdown valve is operated under full boiler pressure once every
operating day. Its gland is a pressure retaining joint that is worked hard, and it is
adjacent the operator's hands during the blowdown. Packing that is at the end of its
adjustment will begin to pass, first as a wisp during operation and then as a continuous
weep. Blowdown valve glands do not fail gracefully. This item is rated Category 4 rather
than Category 3 because the valve currently seals, the weep is intermittent and only on
operation, and the work is properly done with the boiler cold and depressurised rather
than as an emergency.

*Required action.* At the next planned shutdown, isolate and depressurise the boiler, and
replace the gland packing in the slow opening blowdown valve. Inspect the valve seat and
spindle while apart; if the seat is wire-drawn or the spindle is scored, replace the valve
rather than repack it. Clean back the surface oxidation on the shell adjacent the blowdown
connection to bare metal, verify by probe that there is no pitting, and re-paint with a
suitable high temperature coating.

*Required by.* **30 November 2026.** The operator has advised that a plant shutdown is
planned for the second week of October 2026, which is a suitable opportunity and would
close this item well within time.

*Evidence required.* Maintenance record with date, person, parts and a note of the seat
and spindle condition found.

### 10.4 Assessment and recommendations

residual is healthy, the phosphate residual is adequate, the suspended solids are low, and
the condensate iron is very low. The waterside condition found at the 2024 internal
inspection was consistent with a well run programme and there is nothing in this year's
results to change that view.

Two matters are recommended for attention, neither of which is a defect:

1. **Feedwater hardness at the limit.** A hardness of 2 ppm is at the limit and it was nil
   six months ago. Hardness in the feedwater is what forms scale on the heat transfer
   surfaces, and scale is what turns a clean boiler into a fuel-wasting boiler and then
   into an overheated one. The softener should be checked for resin exhaustion or fouling,
   the brine draw and the regeneration timing verified, and a hardness test performed
   immediately after a regeneration to confirm the softener is producing nil hardness when
   freshly regenerated. This should be done at the next monthly service.
2. **Rising total dissolved solids.** See observation 12. Increase surface blowdown
   frequency and investigate the fall in condensate return. A four point fall in condensate
   return means more cold makeup, more chemical, more blowdown and more gas.

The daily operator tests are being performed and recorded, other than on the three days
discussed at Section 11. The daily results recorded by the operator agree closely with the
inspector's results on the day, which indicates the operator is testing correctly and the
reagents are in date.

control tests during the inspection, competently and without prompting.

Formal refresher training was last recorded in March 2024. There is no fixed requirement
for refresher training for this class of licence, but a documented annual refresher — even
a short one run by the boiler service contractor — is good practice and would strengthen
the management system. That is a recommendation, not a finding.

### 11.3 Log review, 1 July 2025 to 24 June 2026

The daily operating log is a bound book kept on the boiler house wall. The prescribed
entries are the date, the time of the check, the steam pressure, the water level observed,
the gauge glass blowdown, the bottom blowdown, the level control test, the boiler water
TDS and alkalinity test results, the chemical dosing pot level, any abnormality, and the
attendant's initials.

**249 operating days fell within the review period. 246 have a complete entry. Three have
no entry at all:**

| Date with no entry | Day | Circumstances established on enquiry |
|---|---|---|
| 13/05/2026 | Wednesday | Primary attendant on leave. Boiler fired and operated by the secondary attendant, who did not complete the log. |
| 14/05/2026 | Thursday | As above. |
| 27/05/2026 | Wednesday | Primary attendant present. Attendant states the checks were performed but the log was not completed on the day and was not back-filled. |

The 246 completed entries are of good quality. They are legible, they are initialled, the
recorded test results are plausible and internally consistent, and abnormalities are
recorded rather than glossed over — there are four entries during the period noting a
level control test that required a second attempt, and one noting a burner lockout on a
cold start that reset and ran normally. A log that records only good news is a log that is
being filled in at the end of the week; this one is not.

### 11.4 What the gaps mean, and what must be done

**What they mean.** Under a limited attendance regime, the log is not paperwork. It is the
evidence that the checks that make unattended operation safe were actually carried out. A
day with no entry is a day on which it cannot be demonstrated that the water level was
observed, that the gauge glass was proved clear, that the level controls were tested, or
that the water chemistry was within limits. On those three days in May 2026 the boiler
operated at 620 kPa with no record that anyone confirmed it was safe to do so.

Three gaps in 249 days is a lapse, not a systemic failure, and no harm resulted — the
boiler was in sound condition before and after, the water chemistry either side of the
gaps was within limits, and no abnormality was recorded on the surrounding days. It is
recorded here as an observation and not as a defect for that reason.

But it is a lapse with three specific consequences the operator should understand:

1. **Regulatory.** The obligation to keep the records is a condition of operating a
   registered boiler under a limited attendance regime. A regulator reviewing this log
   after an incident would treat a gap as a failure of the safety management system, not
   as an administrative oversight.
2. **Insurance.** The machinery breakdown cover on this boiler is conditional on the
   operating records being kept and produced on request. A claim for boiler damage
   following a low water event on a day with no log entry would be difficult to sustain.
3. **Practical.** The gap conceals information. If the boiler had been fired hard on 13
   and 14 May with a level control drifting, nobody would know, because nobody wrote
   anything down.

**Required response.** This is an observation and carries no compliance date, but the
following is expected to be in place at the next inspection:

- a short written procedure stating that the boiler is not to be fired on any day on which
  the daily check regime has not been completed and recorded, signed by the responsible
  officer;
- a documented handover step for use when the primary attendant is on leave, so that the
  secondary attendant knows the log is theirs for the period;
- a weekly review of the log by the responsible officer or the shop manager, initialled, so
  that a gap is found within days rather than at the annual inspection; and
- a note in the log at each of the three gap dates recording that no entry was made, why,
  and what was done about it. Gaps are not to be back-filled with invented readings, which
  would be far worse than the gap itself.
