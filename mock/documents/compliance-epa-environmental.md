## 2. The general environmental duty

### 2.1 What it is

Section 25 of the **Environment Protection Act 2017 (Vic)**, as amended and in
force from 1 July 2021, imposes the general environmental duty (**GED**). In
plain terms: a person engaging in an activity that may give rise to a risk of
harm to human health or the environment from pollution or waste must minimise
those risks, so far as reasonably practicable.

The duty applies to us because we operate a dry cleaning plant using
perchloroethylene and hydrocarbon solvent, we generate industrial waste, and we
discharge to sewer. It applies to the business as the person conducting the
activity, and it also reaches officers of the company. It applies whether or not
we hold any EPA permission, and it is not discharged by holding one.

The 2017 Act replaced the previous licence-and-breach model. Under the old
framework a small dry cleaner could reasonably ask "am I licensed, and have I
been caught doing something wrong?" Under the current framework the question is
"what could go wrong here, and what have I done about it?" That is a different
question and it is the question this manual answers.

### 2.2 It is proactive, not reactive

This is the part most often misunderstood, so it is stated plainly. The GED is
not a rule against causing pollution. It is a duty to identify risk **before**
anything goes wrong and to reduce it. We can breach it on a day when nothing has
spilled, nothing has escaped and nobody has complained, simply by not having
taken a control that was reasonably available to us.

In practice this means the plant is run on the basis that:

- risk is assessed before a change, not after an incident — a new machine, a new
  chemical, a new waste stream or a change to how a task is done triggers a
  written review under clause 10.4;
- controls are maintained whether or not anyone is watching, so a failed bund
  seal, a blocked ventilation intake or an expired respirator cartridge is a
  compliance failure the day it occurs, not the day it causes an escape;
- a near miss is treated as an incident, because the only difference between the
  two is luck, and luck is not a control;
- we keep records that show what we considered and why, because a duty of this
  kind is demonstrated by evidence of a system, not by an absence of complaints.

### 2.3 "Reasonably practicable" in this plant

"Reasonably practicable" is judged on what was known, or ought reasonably to have
been known, at the time, weighing:

1. the likelihood of the harm occurring;
2. the degree of harm that would result;
3. what the person knows, or ought reasonably to know, about the harm and the
   ways of reducing or eliminating it;
4. the availability and suitability of ways to reduce or eliminate the harm; and
5. the cost of reducing or eliminating the harm, which is relevant only where it
   is grossly disproportionate to the risk.

Cost is last on that list and only carries weight when it is grossly
disproportionate. A control is not unreasonable merely because it is
inconvenient, because it slows the shirt run, or because a smaller operator down
the road does not use it. Common practice in the trade is not the test. The test
is what is available and suitable.

**Worked example one — decanting.** Sealed transfer of perchloroethylene from a
200 L drum by pump and closed coupling costs more than open pouring by jug. Open
pouring creates vapour exposure at the operator's breathing zone, drip loss at
every pour and a real chance of a floor spill on every transfer. A drum pump and
couplings are a few hundred dollars against a risk of chronic solvent exposure
and a reportable spill. The cost is not grossly disproportionate. Sealed transfer
is therefore reasonably practicable, and open pouring of perchloroethylene is
prohibited at this site under clause 5.7.

**Worked example two — separator water.** It would be cheaper and quicker to
discharge separator water to the floor waste than to drum it and pay for
quarterly removal as reportable priority waste. The harm — chlorinated solvent
into the sewer and through to the treatment plant, in breach of agreement
TW-4471 and of the Act — is severe and the likelihood on any given discharge is
certain, not speculative. The cost of drumming and removal is a few hundred
dollars a quarter. There is no version of this calculation where discharge is
defensible. Hence the absolute prohibition at clause 7.1.

**Worked example three — the second machine.** The Realstar HM-450 hydrocarbon
machine was bought in August 2023 partly for garments the perc is too aggressive
for, and partly because every garment moved off the perc machine is a garment's
worth of solvent exposure, still residue and waste that does not arise. Reducing
the quantity of the more hazardous substance used is a control in its own right
and sits above containment in the order of preference.

## 3. The regulatory picture

### 3.1 EPA Victoria

The **Environment Protection Act 2017 (Vic)** and the **Environment Protection
Regulations 2021 (Vic)** are the primary instruments. The obligations that bite
on this site are:

- the **general environmental duty** (clause 2);
- the **duty to notify** of a notifiable incident — a pollution incident causing
  or threatening material harm to human health or the environment must be
  notified to EPA as soon as practicable after we become aware of it (clause 9.5);
- the **waste duties** — to characterise waste before it leaves the site, to give
  it only to a person authorised to receive it, and to take reasonable steps to
  ensure it is transported to and received at a lawful place (clause 6);
- the **duty to manage contaminated land**, which arises where we know or ought
  reasonably to know that land we manage is contaminated, and which is the reason
  waste and spill records are kept for the periods at clause 10.1;
- the requirement to hold a **permission** if the activity conducted is a
  prescribed permission activity.

### 3.2 Which permission tier we sit in

EPA permissions run in three tiers by risk. **Registration** is the lowest and
applies to lower-risk prescribed activities. **Permit** sits in the middle.
**Licence** is the highest and applies to the larger scheduled premises —
landfills, waste treatment facilities, major industrial plant. The tier is set by
whether the activity appears as a prescribed permission activity in the schedule
to the Regulations and by the scale thresholds attached to it.

A dry cleaning plant of our size does not meet the thresholds for a licence or a
permit, and dry cleaning as we conduct it is not a prescribed permission
activity. **We therefore hold no EPA permission for the Bendigo plant.** That is
the correct position, not an oversight. It is confirmed at each annual internal
audit, and it is re-checked whenever plant changes — because a change of machine,
solvent volume or waste stream is the kind of thing that could move us across a
threshold, and finding that out afterwards is not acceptable.

Holding no permission does not reduce our obligations. It increases the weight
carried by the general environmental duty, because there is no licence condition
to tell us what "good enough" looks like. Everything in this manual is our answer
to that question, and the reason it is written down is so that the answer can be
tested by someone else.

Separately from our own position, our **waste contractor must be permissioned**.
Sovereign Environmental Services Pty Ltd holds the EPA permission to transport
reportable priority waste. We must satisfy ourselves that it does, and we do so
annually under clause 6.6.

### 3.3 Coliban Water

Discharge to sewer is regulated by **Coliban Water**, not by EPA, under a trade
waste agreement made under the *Water Act 1989* (Vic). We hold trade waste
agreement **TW-4471** for the Bendigo premises. Its conditions are set out at
clause 7.4. A breach of the agreement is a contractual matter with Coliban Water
and can also be an offence, and a discharge of solvent to sewer would additionally
engage the Act.

### 3.4 City of Greater Bendigo

The Council is the responsible authority for planning and the enforcement agency
for public health nuisance. Relevant obligations:

- the tenancy operates under an existing use right for a dry cleaning premises in
  the commercial zone; any intensification of use, new external plant, new flue
  or new signage is checked against the planning scheme before it is ordered, and
  the landlord's consent under the lease is a separate requirement;
- odour, noise and smoke that unreasonably interfere with the enjoyment of
  neighbouring land can be a nuisance under the *Public Health and Wellbeing Act
  2008* (Vic) whether or not they breach an EPA instrument, and the practical
  risk to us is an adjoining retail tenancy rather than a residence;
- commercial noise from the compressor and extraction fans is managed to the
  noise limits in the Environment Protection Regulations, and plant that runs
  outside 7:00–22:00 is restricted to the Pilot K25 compressor only.

### 3.5 Other instruments that touch the plant

- **Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004 (Vic)** and the **Occupational Health
  and Safety Regulations 2017 (Vic)**, Part 4.1 (hazardous substances) — exposure
  standards, health monitoring, labelling, safety data sheets and the hazardous
  substances register. Environmental and occupational obligations overlap heavily
  in a solvent plant, and a control that reduces vapour in the workplace usually
  reduces emission as well.
- **Dangerous Goods Act 1985 (Vic)** and the **Dangerous Goods (Storage and
  Handling) Regulations 2022 (Vic)** — storage, separation, placarding and
  manifest (clause 5.5).
- The **Australian Code for the Transport of Dangerous Goods by Road & Rail** —
  applies to Solvex on delivery and to Sovereign on collection, not to us, since
  we do not transport dangerous goods.
- **AS 1940**, the storage and handling of flammable and combustible liquids,
  used as the design basis for the solvent store.
- **AS 3788**, pressure equipment in-service inspection, which governs the Fulton
  FB-030 and sits in the plant maintenance schedule rather than here.

## 4. Perchloroethylene

### 4.1 What it is and where it is

Perchloroethylene (tetrachloroethylene, "perc") is the solvent charged to the
**Union XL-800**, our 25 kg three-tank machine with integrated still and
refrigerated recovery, serial UXL8-2019-0447, installed March 2019. It is the
single most heavily regulated substance on this site and the reason most of this
manual exists. It is held in the machine's working tanks, in the still, and in up
to two 200 L drums in the solvent store. It exists nowhere else on the premises
and never travels to Kangaroo Flat.

### 4.2 Classification

- UN 1897, Class 6.1 (toxic), packing group III. Hazchem code 2Z.
- GHS classification: Carcinogenicity Category 2; Skin Irritation Category 2; Eye
  Irritation Category 2; Specific Target Organ Toxicity, single exposure,
  Category 3 (narcotic effects); Hazardous to the Aquatic Environment, Chronic
  Category 2.
- Classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer in Group 2A,
  probably carcinogenic to humans.
- Non-flammable. It does not burn, but it decomposes at high temperature to
  hydrogen chloride and phosgene. This is why open flame, hot work and smoking
  are prohibited anywhere in the plant or store, and why the fire brigade is told
  what is in the store on arrival (clause 9.4).
- Density about 1.62 kg/L and only slightly soluble in water. **Spilled perc
  sinks.** It travels down through a drain, through a crack in a slab, through
  soil. It does not sit on the surface waiting to be mopped. This single physical
  fact is why a spill to a drain or to ground is treated as a serious incident
  regardless of volume (clause 9.3).

### 4.3 Exposure standards

The workplace exposure standard adopted in Victoria under Part 4.1 of the
Occupational Health and Safety Regulations 2017 (Vic) is:

| Measure | Standard |
|---|---|
| Time-weighted average, 8 hours | 25 ppm (approximately 170 mg/m³) |
| Short-term exposure limit, 15 minutes | 100 ppm (approximately 680 mg/m³) |

An exposure standard is a ceiling that must not be exceeded. It is not a target
and it is not a safe level — for a substance with a carcinogenicity
classification, exposure is kept as low as reasonably practicable regardless of
the number. Our **internal action level is 10 ppm** as an 8-hour average: if a
monthly survey reading reaches it, work on the affected machine stops, the cause
is found, and the machine does not run again until the reading is back below it
and the fix is recorded.

Perc has an odour threshold well below the exposure standard, which is useful and
misleading in equal measure. Useful, because you can smell a leak long before it
is dangerous. Misleading, because the sense of smell fatigues quickly, so an
operator who has been in the plant for an hour is the last person who will notice
a developing leak. Smell is a prompt to investigate, never an all-clear.

### 4.4 Health effects

Acute over-exposure by inhalation causes dizziness, headache, nausea, loss of
coordination, slurred speech and, at high concentration, unconsciousness. Skin
contact defats the skin and causes dermatitis; repeated contact causes cracking,
splitting and secondary infection. Splashes to the eye cause irritation and
transient corneal damage. Chronic exposure is associated with effects on the
liver, the kidneys and the central nervous system, and with the carcinogenicity
classification above.

Anyone who feels light-headed, unusually tired, nauseous or "flat" while working
near the machine leaves the plant room immediately, tells Peter Nguyen or Karen
Whitfield, and does not return until the cause has been identified. This is not a
matter for judgement and it is not a matter of finishing the load first. The
early symptoms of solvent over-exposure include impaired judgement, which is
exactly why the rule is written as an instruction rather than left to the person
experiencing them.

### 4.5 Health monitoring

Operators with regular perchloroethylene exposure — currently Peter Nguyen and
Marco Rossi — are offered health monitoring at our cost through a registered
medical practitioner experienced in the health effects of solvent exposure. The
monitoring comprises a work and exposure history, a physical examination directed
at the skin and the central nervous system, and liver function testing.

Monitoring is offered on commencement in the role, then at intervals advised by
the practitioner. Records are held for 30 years and are confidential to the
worker and the practitioner; the business holds only the fact and date of the
monitoring and any written advice about fitness for the role.

## 5. Solvent storage

### 5.1 The store

The solvent store is a dedicated, locked, mechanically ventilated compartment at
the rear of the plant, opening off the Bath Lane loading door and separated from
the plant room by a 2-hour fire-rated wall. Access is by key held by Karen
Whitfield and Peter Nguyen only. Nobody else enters unescorted, including
contractors, delivery drivers and the landlord's agent.

The store holds:

- up to **2 × 200 L drums** of perchloroethylene;
- up to **2 × 200 L drums** of hydrocarbon solvent for the Realstar HM-450;
- charge soap, sizing and spotting chemistry in original containers of 20 L or
  less, on a shelf above the bunds;
- the waste containers described at clause 6.4, in a separate bunded waste bay;
- spill Kit 1 (clause 5.8).

Perchloroethylene is Class 6.1 PG III and is not flammable. The hydrocarbon dry
cleaning solvent has a flash point above 61.5°C and is a **C1 combustible liquid**
under AS 1940. The two are stored in separate bunded bays, not in the same bund,
so that a leak of one cannot come into contact with the other and so that the
bund capacity calculation at 5.2 holds independently for each.

### 5.2 Bund capacity — worked calculation

The design rule applied is the **greater of 110% of the largest single container
and 25% of the aggregate stored volume**.

For the perchloroethylene bay holding 2 × 200 L drums:

- Largest single container: 200 L. 110% of 200 L = **220 L**.
- Aggregate stored: 2 × 200 L = 400 L. 25% of 400 L = **100 L**.
- Required capacity is the greater of these two figures: **220 L**.

The perchloroethylene bund is a seamless coated concrete bund with internal
dimensions of 1,800 mm × 1,200 mm and a 160 mm effective wall height. Its
capacity is:

    1.800 m × 1.200 m × 0.160 m = 0.3456 m³ = 345.6 L

**Available capacity 345.6 L against a required capacity of 220 L. Compliant,
with 125.6 L of margin.**

Displacement by the drums themselves is disregarded in this calculation because
the drums sit on a galvanised grated stand above the bund floor rather than on
the floor of the bund. That is why the stand is never removed and why nothing is
ever stored underneath it. If the stand were removed and the drums stood on the
bund floor, the effective capacity would fall by the submerged volume of the
drums and the margin above would shrink accordingly.

The hydrocarbon bay is built to the same dimensions and therefore to the same
345.6 L capacity, against the same required 220 L, and the same calculation
applies. The waste bay is bunded to the same specification, which covers the
maximum waste holding at clause 6.4 (2 × 205 L drums, largest container 205 L,
110% = 225.5 L required).

### 5.3 Bund integrity

- The bunds have **no drain, no valve and no outlet of any kind**. There is no
  circumstance in which a bund is "emptied" by opening something.
- Rainwater cannot enter because the store is internal and the roller door to the
  laneway is a separate compartment.
- Any liquid found in a bund is removed within 24 hours by pump or absorbent into
  a labelled drum and treated as reportable priority waste. It is never hosed,
  never mopped to a floor waste and never tipped outside, whatever it looks like
  and whatever anyone believes it to be.
- The coating is inspected monthly for cracking, lifting, staining or softening
  and the result entered in the bund inspection register. Any defect is reported
  to Karen Whitfield the same day and the affected bay is emptied of drums until
  it is repaired.

### 5.4 Separation and access

- Minimum 3 m separation between the solvent store and any ignition source,
  switchboard, hot work position or the boiler.
- The store is not used as a thoroughfare and holds no combustible packaging,
  cardboard, poly rolls or garment stock. The temptation to use it as overflow
  storage in the September–November peak is specifically anticipated and
  specifically prohibited.
- A clear 900 mm access path is maintained to both bunds and to the spill kit.
- The store door is kept closed and locked except during a transfer, a delivery
  or a collection.

### 5.5 Signage and ventilation

Signage at the store door comprises: a Class 6.1 diamond; a C1 combustible liquid
sign; "No smoking, no naked flame"; "Authorised persons only"; the duplicate
safety data sheet folder; and the Appendix D emergency contact board.

Ventilation is dedicated mechanical extraction taking from **low level**, because
perchloroethylene vapour is significantly heavier than air and collects at floor
level rather than rising. Extraction runs at not less than 10 air changes per
hour, discharges above the roof line clear of the neighbouring tenancy's intake,
and is interlocked to run whenever the store light is on. The interlock is tested
at each monthly bund inspection by the simple method of turning the light on and
listening for the fan.

### 5.6 Manifest and placarding thresholds

Under the Dangerous Goods (Storage and Handling) Regulations 2022 (Vic),
placarding and manifest duties are triggered by exceeding the **placarding
quantity** and the **manifest quantity** respectively for the class of dangerous
goods held. For Class 6.1 packing group III liquids those thresholds run to
thousands and tens of thousands of litres. For C1 combustible liquids the
equivalent thresholds are higher again.

Maximum quantities held on this site:

| Substance | Class | Maximum held |
|---|---|---|
| Perchloroethylene | 6.1 PG III | 400 L in store, plus machine charge |
| Hydrocarbon solvent | C1 combustible | 400 L in store, plus machine charge |
| Process chemistry (assorted) | Various, 20 L or less each | About 60 L total |

**This site does not reach the placarding quantity or the manifest quantity for
any class held. We are not required to placard the premises or to keep a
dangerous goods manifest.**

We keep a manifest-style **chemical register** (Appendix B) anyway, and we display
the outer door placard anyway. The reason is practical rather than legal: a fire
crew attending at 2 a.m. should not have to take our word for what is inside, and
a compliance position that depends on a threshold is worth re-checking every time
the plant changes. Doing more than the threshold requires is a control under
clause 2.3. It is not an admission that the threshold is met.

### 5.7 Drum handling and decanting

**Handling.** Drums are delivered by Solvex Chemicals Australia to the Bath Lane
door and moved on a drum trolley. They are never rolled on the rim and never
lifted by the bung. They are stored upright, bungs uppermost, on the grated stand
within the bund, and are never stacked. Every drum is labelled on receipt with
the date received and a sequential drum number, and that number is recorded on
the waste log when the drum is finally emptied, so that any drum can be traced
from delivery to disposal.

Empty perchloroethylene drums are not rinsed, not cut open, not reused for any
purpose and not sold for scrap. They are returned to Solvex on the next delivery
with the bungs closed, or removed by Sovereign as reportable priority waste. An
"empty" 200 L perc drum still holds residue and vapour and is treated as full for
handling purposes.

A drum is rejected on delivery if it is damaged, corroded, weeping, unlabelled,
mislabelled or arrives without a current safety data sheet. A rejected drum stays
on the truck. Peter Nguyen or Karen Whitfield may reject a delivery; nobody else
signs for one.

**Decanting.** Transfer of perchloroethylene from drum to machine is by **closed
system only**: a dedicated hand-operated drum pump with a fixed suction tube and a
closed coupling to the machine fill point, positioned over the bund, with a drip
tray under the coupling. The following are prohibited:

- open pouring or transfer by jug, funnel or bucket;
- gravity siphoning;
- use of a pump that has been used for another chemical;
- transfer while the store extraction is not running;
- transfer by a person who is alone in the building;
- transfer by anyone other than Peter Nguyen, or Marco Rossi under the training
  at clause 11.2.

Personal protective equipment for a transfer: chemical splash goggles, face
shield, apron, and **laminate or Viton gloves — not nitrile**, which
perchloroethylene swells and permeates in minutes. Gloves are inspected before
each use and discarded at the first sign of swelling, stiffening or
discolouration.

Every transfer is entered in the solvent transfer log with the date, drum number,
litres transferred and operator. The log is what tells us our annual consumption,
which is what tells us whether the machine has developed a slow loss.

## 6. Industrial waste

### 6.1 Terminology

Staff still say "PIW" and "prescribed industrial waste", which was the term used
before 1 July 2021. The current legal term under the Environment Protection Act
2017 (Vic) is **priority waste**, and the streams we generate are **reportable
priority waste**, which must be tracked. Both terms appear on our paperwork, our
labels and our older files, and they mean the same thing here. All references to
PIW in this manual are references to reportable priority waste.

### 6.2 What we generate

| Stream | Source | Typical volume | Container |
|---|---|---|---|
| Still bottoms and residues | Union XL-800 still, Bowe MiniStill | 55–70 L per quarter | 205 L drum, lined |
| Spent cartridge filters | Union XL-800 filter housings | 8–12 units per quarter | 205 L drum |
| Separator water | Water separator, Union XL-800 | 40–60 L per quarter | 60 L drum |
| Contaminated rags and absorbents | Spotting board, spill clean-up | about half a drum per quarter | 205 L drum |
| Spent carbon | Union XL-800 adsorber bed | As changed | 205 L drum |
| Hydrocarbon still residues | Realstar HM-450 | 20–30 L per quarter | 60 L drum |
| Empty solvent drums | Solvent store | 4–6 per year | As-is, bungs closed |

Volumes rise in the September–November peak in proportion to throughput, which
runs to about 1,400 garments a week against an average of about 1,050. The
November collection is consistently the largest of the year and is booked with
that in mind.

### 6.3 Classification and codes

Waste is characterised before it leaves the site. Characterisation for our
streams is based on the supplier's safety data sheets, the process knowledge
recorded in this manual, and periodic laboratory analysis arranged through
Sovereign Environmental Services. It is reviewed at least annually and on any
change of solvent or process chemistry.

On the current characterisation:

- Perchloroethylene still bottoms, spent cartridge filters, spent carbon,
  separator water, and perc-contaminated rags and absorbents are classified as
  **Category B** reportable priority waste, consigned under waste code **N220 —
  halogenated organic solvents and residues**.
- Hydrocarbon still residues are consigned under **N205 — non-halogenated organic
  solvents and residues**, Category C.

If the classification of a container is in doubt — for example a mixed container
of absorbent used on both machines — the waste is held and consigned at the
higher category until the position is resolved. **We do not guess downwards.** The
cost of consigning at Category B what might have been Category C is a few dollars
a drum. The cost of the reverse is an offence.

### 6.4 Packaging, labelling and storage limits

- Containers are UN-marked steel drums in sound condition with closed lids and
  ring clamps. Bags, buckets, wheelie bins, cut-down drums and old chemical
  containers are not used, whatever is to hand.
- Every container is labelled on the day it is first filled with: the generator
  (Main Street Dry Cleaners, Shop 4, 118 Hargreaves Street, Bendigo VIC 3550);
  the waste description; the waste code; the Class 6.1 diamond; and the date
  first filled. The date first filled is what starts the storage clock.
- Containers are held in the bunded waste bay in the solvent store. Never in the
  laneway, never in the retail front, never in the plant room aisle, never
  outside overnight, never on the loading dock "ready for pickup tomorrow".
- Maximum held on site at any time: **2 × 205 L drums** of Category B waste and
  **1 × 60 L drum** of separator water, plus the hydrocarbon residue drum. If a
  container fills before the scheduled collection, an interim collection is booked
  under clause 6.6. Waste is never transferred between containers to make room,
  and a full drum is never topped up by compressing its contents.
- Maximum storage time on site is the interval to the next scheduled collection,
  and in no case more than 12 months from the date first filled.

### 6.5 Tracking, duty of care, and who the generator is

Every consignment of reportable priority waste is tracked. Sovereign
Environmental Services raises the electronic waste record; Peter Nguyen confirms
the description, code, container count and volume at the point of pickup and
signs; a copy is filed by Nadia Farrell the same day and entered in the waste
collection log at Appendix C. Where a paper waste transport certificate is used
as a backup, the same details are recorded and the certificate number is entered
in the log.

Two things must be understood by anyone signing:

1. **We remain the waste generator in law.** Handing waste to a transporter does
   not transfer the obligation. If our waste is dumped, mis-described, or
   delivered to a facility not authorised to receive it, we are exposed. Our duty
   is to take reasonable steps to ensure the waste is transported to, and received
   at, a lawful place. That is why we check the permission and the destination
   rather than assuming both.
2. **Signing is a statement of fact.** The description and the volume on the
   record must match what is actually going onto the truck. If they do not, it is
   corrected before the truck leaves. Not after, and not by a phone call the
   following week.

### 6.6 The collection arrangement

Collections are quarterly by **Sovereign Environmental Services Pty Ltd**, an EPA
permissioned transporter of reportable priority waste. The arrangement is made
through and included in our supply agreement with Solvex Chemicals Australia,
which covers residue removal as part of the solvent supply.

Collections run in the second week of **February, May, August and November**, from
the Bath Lane door, between 7:00 and 9:00 so that the laneway is clear of
customer traffic and the retail front is not affected. Before each collection
Peter Nguyen completes the pre-collection checklist: container condition,
labelling legible and complete, lids and clamps secure, volumes estimated, access
path clear, and the previous collection's paperwork filed.

Annually in May, Nadia Farrell obtains and files current evidence of Sovereign's
EPA permission and public liability cover, and checks the destination facility
named on the records against the scope of that permission. If either lapses,
collections stop until it is current — waste accumulating on site is a lesser
problem than waste leaving site with an unpermissioned carrier.

An interim collection may be booked at any time on 5 business days' notice, and
is booked without hesitation whenever a container is approaching full ahead of
schedule.

### 6.7 Record retention

Waste records are kept for **7 years** from the date of consignment. This exceeds
the minimum period and is deliberate: a contaminated land question, an insurance
question or a purchaser's due diligence enquiry can arise long after the waste
has gone, and the records are the only thing that answers it. Records are held
electronically with a paper copy of each signed record in the annual waste file.

## 7. Wastewater and trade waste

### 7.1 The separator

The Union XL-800 discharges condensed water through an integrated separator which
gravity-separates perchloroethylene from water by density and returns the solvent
to the working tank. Separation is never complete. The separated water phase
**still contains dissolved perchloroethylene** at concentrations well above
anything acceptable to sewer.

**Separator water is drained to a labelled 60 L drum and consigned as reportable
priority waste. It is never discharged to sewer, to a floor waste, to the
laneway, to a garden, to a stormwater pit or to the ground. There is no exception
to this, no volume small enough to be an exception, and no circumstance in which
a supervisor, a service technician or a contractor can authorise otherwise.**

The separator is checked at the start of each week for water carryover, sludge
build-up and correct weir level, and the check is recorded. A separator that is
carrying over is a machine fault and is reported the same day.

### 7.2 What may go to sewer

Permitted under agreement TW-4471:

- wash and rinse water from the three Speed Queen washers and from the wet
  cleaning process;
- condensate from the Fulton FB-030 boiler and steam lines, after cooling and pH
  correction;
- boiler blowdown, after cooling;
- hand basin, toilet, kitchenette and floor washdown water from the retail front
  and from the plant floor, where the floor is free of solvent.

### 7.3 What must never go to sewer

Perchloroethylene or hydrocarbon solvent in any quantity; separator water; still
bottoms or residues; spent filters or filter drainings; spotting chemistry
concentrates; the contents of any bund; spill absorbent wash-out; and
solvent-contaminated water from any source at all, including the water used to
rinse a spotting board or a spotting gun.

The practical failure mode is not somebody tipping a drum down a drain. It is
somebody rinsing something in a sink because rinsing things in a sink is what you
do. Hence the rule is stated as a list of substances rather than a list of
actions.

### 7.4 Agreement TW-4471 conditions

| Parameter | Limit |
|---|---|
| Maximum daily discharge volume | 4.5 kL |
| Maximum instantaneous flow rate | 1.5 L/s |
| pH | 6.0 – 10.0 |
| Temperature at the boundary | 38°C maximum |
| Suspended solids | 600 mg/L |
| Biochemical oxygen demand | 600 mg/L |
| Oil and grease | 100 mg/L |
| Chlorinated hydrocarbons | Not detectable — nil permitted |
| Discharge hours | Trading hours only |

The agreement also requires that the trade waste connection point remains
accessible for inspection and sampling at any time without notice; that any
change to process or chemistry that could affect the discharge is notified to
Coliban Water in advance; and that any accidental discharge outside the limits is
reported. It is reviewed on renewal and is a standing item at the annual internal
audit.

Boiler condensate and blowdown are the two streams most likely to put us outside
the temperature and pH limits, which is why both are cooled and, in the case of
condensate, pH-corrected before discharge.

### 7.5 Sampling

Coliban Water samples at the inspection point at its discretion and without
notice. We take our own sample annually in March, through a NATA-accredited
laboratory, tested for pH, suspended solids, BOD, oil and grease and chlorinated
hydrocarbons. Results are filed with the agreement. Any exceedance, and any
detection at all of chlorinated hydrocarbons, is investigated in writing and the
investigation is filed with the result.

## 8. Air emissions and ventilation

### 8.1 Plant room ventilation

The plant room is mechanically ventilated at not less than 10 air changes per
hour, with low-level extraction at the Union XL-800 and at the spotting board and
make-up air drawn from the front of the tenancy. Extraction discharges above the
roof line, away from the neighbouring tenancy's intake. Fans are interlocked to
the plant room lighting circuit and run for 15 minutes after shutdown, so that
the room is not left to stand with vapour in it overnight.

Intakes and grilles are checked monthly for blockage. The most common failure by
a wide margin is garment stock, cartons or poly rolls stacked against a low-level
grille during a busy week, which is why the 600 mm zone in front of every grille
is floor-marked in yellow and kept clear. A blocked low-level grille disables
precisely the extraction that matters most, because that is where the vapour is.

### 8.2 Machine vapour containment

The Union XL-800 is a closed-loop dry-to-dry machine with refrigerated vapour
recovery and a secondary **carbon adsorber** polishing the air stream before the
door interlock releases. The door cannot be opened until the cycle has completed
its deodorising phase and the vapour concentration in the drum has fallen below
the machine's set point.

**The interlock is never bypassed, jumpered or mechanically defeated.** Doing so
is a dismissible matter and is recorded as such in the employment documents. A
machine that will not release its door has told you something; the answer is a
service call, not a screwdriver.

The carbon adsorber bed is changed on the schedule in the machine manual, or
earlier if odour is detected on unloading. Since the 2023 EPA site visit noted at
clause 10.3 the interval has been shortened from the manufacturer's maximum.
Spent carbon is consigned as reportable priority waste under N220.

The leak points, in the order they actually fail, are: door gaskets, still lid
seal, filter housing seals, pump glands, hose couplings and the button trap. They
are checked weekly and any leak, however small, is repaired before the next load
runs. There is no such thing as an acceptable drip. A drip is a measurable
quantity of solvent leaving the closed loop and a measurable quantity of vapour
entering the room.

### 8.3 Monitoring

- **Weekly:** visual and olfactory leak check of the points above, recorded.
- **Monthly:** survey of the plant room and store with a photoionisation
  detector, readings recorded at six fixed points — machine door, still, filter
  housing, spotting board, store low level, store door. Action level 10 ppm
  (clause 4.3). All six points, every month, on the pre-printed form.
- **Annually:** ventilation flow verification at each extraction point by the
  mechanical services contractor, report filed.
- **On any complaint:** immediate survey, written record, and a call back to the
  complainant within one business day, whether or not the survey finds anything.

### 8.4 Boiler combustion products

The Fulton FB-030 vertical gas-fired steam boiler, 30 hp, 690 kPa MAWP,
registration VIC-PV-118-4470, installed 2014 and refurbished 2021, burns natural
gas and produces carbon dioxide, water vapour, oxides of nitrogen and — if
incorrectly tuned or starved of air — carbon monoxide.

Controls: an annual combustion tune and flue gas analysis by the service agent
with the report filed; a flue terminating above the roof line and clear of
adjoining intakes; and a monthly visual check for soot staining, flue
discolouration or a yellow-tipped flame, any of which stops the boiler until the
service agent attends.

The AS 3788 external inspection by Goldfields Plant Inspection Services, and the
pressure vessel registration expiring 30 June 2027, are safety obligations. They
sit in the plant maintenance schedule, not in this manual, and are noted here only
so that nobody assumes the annual combustion tune covers them. It does not.

## 9. Spill and incident response

The order is always the same: **people first, containment second, clean-up third,
records last.** Nobody is expected to protect the floor at the cost of breathing
perc vapour, and nobody will be criticised for evacuating a spill that turned out
to be small.

### 9.1 Small spill — under 5 litres, contained inside a bund or on a sealed floor

1. Stop the source. Close the bung, stop the pump, shut the valve.
2. Tell everyone in the plant. Move anyone not needed to the retail front.
3. Confirm the extraction is running. Open the Bath Lane door if the spill is in
   the store.
4. Put on goggles, laminate gloves and coverall from Kit 1.
5. Ring the spill with absorbent socks, then cover with pads working from the
   outside in, so that nothing is pushed outward.
6. Collect saturated absorbent into a labelled PIW bag, then into the salvage
   drum. Close and label the drum with the date first filled.
7. Ventilate for at least 30 minutes before normal work resumes.
8. Restock the kit within 48 hours and complete the Appendix A spill report.

### 9.2 Large spill — over 5 litres, or any spill outside a bunded area

1. Steps 1 to 3 above, then **evacuate the plant room and the store**. Do not
   attempt clean-up on your own.
2. Ring Peter Nguyen. If he is not on site, ring Karen Whitfield.
3. Close the internal door between the plant and the retail front. Consider
   closing the shop; a spill in the plant is a good enough reason to lose an
   afternoon's counter trade.
4. Cover the nearest floor waste and any drain with a drain cover before anything
   else, including before starting to absorb.
5. Two people minimum, both in A1P2 respirators, goggles and laminate gloves.
   Contain with socks and vermiculite, working from the perimeter inwards.
6. Do not hose. Do not use water. Do not sweep dry absorbent in a way that raises
   dust.
7. Recover to the salvage drum. Consign as N220 Category B.
8. Make the notifications at clause 9.5. Complete the spill report the same day.

### 9.3 Spill reaching a drain, the laneway or soil

Treat as a serious incident regardless of volume. Perchloroethylene is denser
than water and will move down through a drain or through soil rather than staying
on the surface where it can be recovered.

1. Evacuate as at 9.2. Ring 000 if anyone is affected, or if the quantity is
   unknown and continuing.
2. Ring Karen Whitfield immediately. She, or Peter Nguyen if she is unreachable,
   makes the notifications at 9.5 — not the person holding the mop.
3. Block further entry to the drain. Bund the laneway kerb line with socks to
   stop movement toward the stormwater pit.
4. **Do not flush the drain with water.** Water pushes it further and turns a
   contained problem into a catchment problem.
5. Photograph the area before clean-up if it is safe to do so. The photographs
   matter to the notification, to the insurer and to any later assessment.
6. Ring Sovereign Environmental Services for an emergency attendance, and if the
   spill is to soil or beyond our boundary, an environmental consultant to advise
   on assessment.

### 9.4 Fire, or a gas or vapour emergency

Ring 000. Evacuate to the assembly point on the Bath Lane side. Account for
everyone against the day's roster, including the drop store driver if he is on
site.

Tell the attending officer three things: that the store holds perchloroethylene;
that it is non-flammable but decomposes in a fire to hydrogen chloride and
phosgene; and where the store is. The Appendix D board at the store door carries
the same information in writing for them.

### 9.5 Notification triggers

| Trigger | Who must be told | By when |
|---|---|---|
| Pollution incident causing or threatening material harm to human health or the environment | EPA Victoria, 24-hour pollution hotline 1300 372 842 | As soon as practicable after becoming aware |
| Any solvent, separator water or prohibited substance entering the sewer | Coliban Water, 1300 363 200 | Immediately, and in any case within 2 hours |
| Spill to soil, stormwater, the laneway or beyond the boundary | EPA Victoria; City of Greater Bendigo | Same day |
| Notifiable incident involving death, serious injury or exposure requiring medical treatment | WorkSafe Victoria, 13 23 60 | Immediately, and the site preserved |
| Fire, unknown or continuing release, or anyone overcome | 000 | Immediately |
| Any incident above | Karen Whitfield | Immediately |
| Any incident above | Hargreaves & Locke Insurance Brokers, Bendigo | Within 48 hours |
| Any incident involving waste | Sovereign Environmental Services | Same day |
| Any incident affecting the tenancy or the building | Castleton Property Group, for the Rowe Family Trust | Same day |

Where there is doubt about whether a notification threshold is met, **we notify**.
The cost of an unnecessary notification is a phone call. The cost of a missed one
is an offence, and a much harder conversation later about what we knew and when.

### 9.6 The incident record

Every spill, however small, and every near miss is recorded on the Appendix A
form within 24 hours by the person who dealt with it. Karen Whitfield reviews and
signs every form.

The record must state what happened; the substance and quantity; what was done;
who was told and when; what waste was generated and under what consignment; the
root cause; and what has changed so that it does not happen again. A form that
says "operator error" and nothing else is returned unsigned, because operator
error is a description of the last event in the chain, not a cause.

Incident forms are reviewed as a set at the annual internal audit to see whether
the same thing keeps happening in a different disguise.
