# PART B — POLICY WORDING

This Part sets out the operative cover. Where a defined term is used it appears in the
Definitions at the end of B10. The Schedule at Part A prevails over this Part where the
two conflict.

## B1. Section 1 — Property / Material Damage

### 1.1 What is insured

Meridian will pay for physical loss of or damage to the insured property at the situations
shown in the Schedule occurring during the period of cover and caused by a peril not
excluded. Cover is on an accidental damage basis: the insured does not have to identify a
named peril, but does have to show that an identified event caused the damage.

### 1.2 Insured property

- **Landlord's fixtures and tenant's improvements** — the fitout, partitions, floor
  coverings, ceilings, lighting, shopfront joinery, plumbing and services installed by or
  at the cost of the insured, and the landlord's fixtures for which the insured is
  responsible under the lease. The interest of the landlord is noted; the landlord insures
  the building structure itself and the insured does not.
- **Plant and machinery** as itemised at A8. Each item is separately insured for the sum
  shown against it. A sum insured against one item is not available to make up a shortfall
  against another item.
- **Contents**, being the fitout, furniture, fittings, racking, conveyors not itemised,
  office and point of sale equipment.
- **Stock and materials** at the cost of replacement to the insured, without allowance for
  profit. Solvent held in the bunded store is stock.

### 1.3 Basis of settlement

Insured property is settled on a reinstatement basis: the cost of replacing or repairing
the property with property of the same kind, capacity and specification, without deduction
for depreciation. Where the insured does not proceed to reinstate, settlement is on an
indemnity basis, being replacement cost less an allowance for age, wear and condition.

Where an item of plant is no longer manufactured, Meridian will settle on the cost of the
nearest equivalent current model of the same capacity and specification, and will not pay
for a capacity or specification upgrade beyond that. Where a machine can be economically
repaired it will be repaired; a machine is a total loss only where the cost of repair
exceeds its replacement cost, or where the manufacturer's authorised agent certifies that
it cannot be returned to a safe and serviceable condition.

Reinstatement must be commenced with reasonable despatch and completed within 12 months of
the date of damage, or such further period as Meridian allows in writing.

### 1.4 Removal of debris

Meridian will pay the reasonable cost of removing, storing and lawfully disposing of
debris, dismantling damaged property, and cleaning up drains, pits and the trade waste
connection within the situation, up to $50,000. Where debris is contaminated with solvent
or solvent residue, disposal must be through a lawfully permissioned waste contractor, and
the cost of that disposal is included within this limit. This benefit does not extend to
the cost of removing or remediating contaminated soil or groundwater.

### 1.5 Extra cost of reinstatement

Where the insured is required by a statutory authority, a building regulation or the terms
of the lease to reinstate to a standard higher than the standard that existed before the
damage, Meridian will pay the additional cost, up to 20% of the amount otherwise payable
and not more than $100,000. This benefit does not apply to a requirement notified to the
insured before the damage occurred, and does not apply to any part of the property that
was not damaged.

By way of example, where a damaged boiler flue must on replacement be relocated or
re-sized to meet a current standard, the additional cost of doing so falls within this
benefit; where the boiler was already the subject of a written direction to alter the flue
before the damage, it does not.

### 1.6 Average / co-insurance

**This is the condition that most often reduces a property claim, so read it.**

If at the time of loss the actual replacement value of the insured property in a category
exceeds the sum insured shown for that category, the insured is treated as its own insurer
for the difference and bears a rateable proportion of the loss. Where the sum insured is
at least 90% of the actual replacement value, no reduction is applied.

Worked example. If the press line at item P5 is insured for $96,000 and its actual
replacement cost at the time of loss is $120,000, the sum insured is 80% of value, which
is below the 90% threshold. A $40,000 partial loss would be settled at
$40,000 x 96,000 / 120,000 = $32,000, less the excess of $2,500, so $29,500.

Average is applied item by item for itemised plant and category by category for contents
and stock. It applies to a partial loss as well as to a total loss, and it is the partial
loss where it bites hardest, because the insured is often unaware that the sum insured has
fallen behind replacement cost until the claim is assessed.

The insured should review each sum insured before each renewal, after any plant purchase
or replacement, and after any material change in the cost of imported equipment or the
exchange rate. Meridian will accept a written valuation from the manufacturer's authorised
agent as evidence of replacement cost.

### 1.7 Section 1 exclusions

Meridian does not cover under this Section:

1. wear, tear, gradual deterioration, rust, corrosion, mould, damp, dry rot, or the action
   of light, vermin or insects;
2. faulty workmanship or faulty materials, other than resulting damage to other property;
3. mechanical or electrical breakdown, which is dealt with under Section 5;
4. damage to a machine caused by its own operation on the goods within it, including
   damage caused by a foreign object left in a garment;
5. theft not accompanied by forcible and violent entry to or exit from a building, except
   theft by an identified person lawfully on the premises;
6. damage to customers' goods, which is dealt with under Section 4;
7. loss of solvent by evaporation, leakage or absence not attributable to an identified
   event, and any inventory shortfall of solvent;
8. damage to plant while it is being installed, dismantled or relocated by anyone other
   than the manufacturer's authorised service agent;
9. damage to electrical equipment by electrical current, arcing or self-heating, other
   than resulting fire damage to other property;
10. settling, shrinkage or expansion of buildings or foundations; and
11. the cost of repairing a defect that caused escaping water, though the resulting water
    damage is covered.

## B2. Section 2 — Business Interruption

### 2.1 What is insured

Where the insured suffers damage that is covered under Section 1 or Section 5, Meridian
will pay the resulting loss of gross profit and the increased cost of working during the
indemnity period. Cover under this Section depends on a claim being payable under Section
1 or Section 5; if the property claim fails, the interruption claim fails with it.

### 2.2 Sum insured and indemnity period

Gross profit sum insured $420,000. Indemnity period 12 months, commencing on the date the
damage occurs. Gross profit is turnover less the cost of solvent, chemistry, consumables,
packaging and outsourced processing. Wages of all employees are included within gross
profit and are not separately insured.

The 12 month indemnity period is chosen deliberately. A total loss of the Bendigo plant
would require the tenancy to be made good by the landlord, new plant to be ordered from
overseas, a boiler to be installed, commissioned and registered as plant, and the customer
base to be rebuilt. Twelve months is the period over which the insured's earnings would
reasonably be expected to be affected, and it is not a period during which the loss must
be resolved.

### 2.3 Additional increased cost of working

In addition to the increased cost of working recoverable under 2.1, Meridian will pay up
to $100,000 for additional expenditure reasonably and necessarily incurred to avoid or
reduce a reduction in turnover, whether or not that expenditure is recovered by the
resulting saving. This is the benefit that pays for outsourcing work to another plant,
hiring a mobile boiler, air freighting a replacement component, or paying overtime to
clear a backlog.

The economic limit test that applies to ordinary increased cost of working — that
expenditure is recoverable only up to the loss it saves — does not apply to this benefit.

### 2.4 The dry cleaner's exposure — boiler and machine outage

The insured's plant has two single points of failure: the steam boiler, on which every
press, finisher, still and the shirt unit depends, and the dry cleaning machines.

**The boiler.** Where the Fulton FB-030 suffers damage covered under Section 1 or a
breakdown covered under Section 5, the whole finishing line stops. Garments can still be
cleaned but cannot be finished, and the plant cannot deliver. Meridian records that in
that circumstance the insured may, without further authority, incur additional increased
cost of working under 2.3 for:

- the hire, delivery, connection, fuelling and removal of a temporary packaged steam
  boiler, for up to 60 days;
- the temporary gas or fuel connection and any temporary flue required for it;
- the outsourcing of pressing and finishing to another plant, including the cost of
  transporting garments to and from that plant; and
- overtime and additional casual labour to clear the resulting backlog.

The insured must notify Meridian within 48 hours of committing to hire. Where the boiler
is out of service for more than 14 days, Meridian will meet with the insured and the
broker to agree a plan for the balance of the indemnity period.

**The dry cleaning machines.** Where the Union XL-800 is out of service and the Realstar
HM-450 remains serviceable, or the reverse, the loss is assessed on the reduced throughput
actually achievable on the surviving machine, and not on a total cessation. The insured is
expected to reschedule work onto the surviving machine, and to advise customers of
extended turnaround, before outsourcing. Where the fabric or trim of a garment makes the
surviving machine unsuitable, outsourcing that garment is reasonable and is recoverable.

**The Kangaroo Flat drop store.** Damage at Situation 1 that stops production also stops
the earnings of Situation 2, because Situation 2 has no plant. Loss of turnover at
Situation 2 arising from damage at Situation 1 is recoverable under this Section.

### 2.5 Other benefits under this Section

- **Claims preparation costs** — the reasonable professional fees of an accountant or loss
  consultant engaged by the insured to prepare and substantiate a claim, up to $25,000.
  Fees payable on a contingency or percentage of settlement basis are not covered.
- **Prevention of access** — loss following damage to property within 100 m of a situation
  that prevents access to it, up to 12 weeks.
- **Public utilities** — loss following damage at the premises of the supplier of
  electricity, gas or water within 20 km of a situation, excess 24 hours.
- **Customers' and suppliers' premises** — loss following damage at the premises of a
  named customer or supplier, up to $50,000.
- **Loss of attraction** — loss following damage to a property within the same shopping
  centre as Situation 2 that materially reduces customer traffic, up to $25,000.
- **Rewriting of records** — the cost of reconstructing the garment tracking records and
  customer database, up to $15,000.

### 2.6 Time excess

72 hours. No claim is payable under this Section for an interruption that is made good
within 72 hours of the damage occurring. Where an interruption exceeds 72 hours, the loss
is calculated from the date of damage and the 72 hours is not deducted twice.

## B3. Section 3 — Public and Products Liability

Meridian will indemnify the insured against all sums the insured becomes legally liable to
pay as compensation for personal injury or property damage happening during the period of
cover and caused by an occurrence in connection with the business.

**Limit of indemnity: $20,000,000 any one occurrence**, and in the aggregate during any one
period of cover in respect of products liability. Legal defence costs and the costs of an
inquest or inquiry incurred with Meridian's written consent are payable in addition to the
limit.

Cover extends to the insured's liability arising from:

- the pickup and delivery of garments, including work performed at a customer's premises;
- the sale or supply of goods, including hangers, covers and retail products;
- the use and occupation of the situations, including liability to a visitor or contractor;
- the insured's liability assumed under the Bendigo lease and the Kangaroo Flat licence,
  to the extent the liability would have existed without the agreement;
- goods sold or supplied and no longer in the insured's control; and
- the insured's participation in a market, fair or community event.

**Excluded under this Section:** liability for damage to property in the insured's physical
or legal control, which is dealt with under Section 4; liability to an employee arising out
of employment, which is the subject of workers compensation; liability arising from the
ownership or use of a registered vehicle, other than the loading and unloading of it;
liability for the cost of recalling, repairing, replacing or reperforming the insured's own
work or product; liability for pure financial loss unconnected with injury or damage; fines,
penalties, punitive and exemplary damages; liability arising from asbestos; liability
arising from a professional opinion given for a fee; and pollution liability except as
provided by General Exclusion 4.

## B4. Section 4 — Bailee's / Customers' Goods Liability

**This Section is the core of the cover for a dry cleaner. It is the Section under which
most claims are made, and the Section whose conditions the counter staff must know.**

### 4.1 What is insured

Meridian will indemnify the insured for loss of or damage to customers' goods while those
goods are in the insured's physical custody or under the insured's control for the purpose
of cleaning, wet cleaning, laundering, pressing, finishing, alteration, repair, storage or
delivery, and while those goods are:

- at Situation 1;
- at Situation 2;
- in transit between Situation 1 and Situation 2 in the insured's vehicle;
- in transit to or from a customer's address in the insured's vehicle; or
- at the premises of a specialist subcontractor engaged by the insured for leather
  cleaning, wedding gown preservation or curtain cleaning, for up to 30 days.

Cover applies whether or not the insured is legally liable for the loss or damage. This
Section is a first party cover on the goods of others, not a liability cover, and Meridian
will not require the customer to prove negligence. That does not make the policy a
guarantee of the insured's work; the exclusions at 4.3 are what confine it.

Customers' goods held for a customer under a storage or seasonal storage arrangement are
insured, provided the goods have been cleaned before storage and the storage arrangement
is recorded against a docket.

### 4.2 Limits and excess

| Limit | Amount |
|---|---|
| Any one event | $50,000 |
| Any one garment, or any one matched suit or matched set | $25,000 |
| Goods in transit in the insured's vehicle, any one event | $20,000 |
| Goods at a subcontractor's premises, any one event | $15,000 |
| Goods held in seasonal storage, any one event | $20,000 |
| Mysterious disappearance, any one event and in the aggregate in any one period of cover | $5,000 |
| Excess, each and every event | $2,500 |

A matched suit, a two piece or three piece outfit, or a set of curtains from one room is
treated as one garment for the purposes of the inner limit. Where one piece of a matched
set is damaged beyond repair, Meridian will pay for the whole set less an allowance for
the wear of the undamaged pieces, and the undamaged pieces become Meridian's property.

The any one event limit is the practical constraint. A conveyor holding 400 garments has a
replacement value well above $50,000. A fire that destroys the conveyor is one event, and
the most Meridian will pay for the garments on it is $50,000. The insured should
understand this and should not tell a customer otherwise.

### 4.3 What is not insured under this Section

Meridian does not pay for:

1. **Inherent vice** — a defect, weakness or characteristic within the garment itself,
   including latent weakness in fibre, an unstable dye, a fusible interlining that
   delaminates, adhesive failure in beading or trim, a deteriorated leather finish, a
   coated or bonded fabric that separates, or a fabric weakened by perspiration,
   deodorant, sunlight, atmospheric fume or previous cleaning.
2. **Colour change, colour loss, colour bleed or colour migration** occurring through no
   fault of the insured, including where the garment was cleaned by the process indicated
   on the care label, and including dye transfer between components of the same garment.
3. **Wear and tear, gradual deterioration, shrinkage or relaxation** consistent with the
   age and use of the garment, and the ordinary loss of finish, glaze, nap or pleating.
4. **Damage arising from correctly following the care label**, where the label was legible,
   was attached to the garment on intake, and specified the process used. This exclusion
   does not apply if the insured departed from the label, or if the insured knew or should
   reasonably have known that the label was wrong for the fabric and proceeded without an
   at-risk authorisation.
5. **Mysterious disappearance** beyond the $5,000 limit at 4.2 — meaning the loss of a
   garment which cannot be attributed to an identified event, and which is discovered only
   on stocktake, reconciliation or customer enquiry.
6. **Goods left uncollected for more than 3 months** from the date the garment was ready
   for collection, where the insured has given the customer at least one written or SMS
   notice that the garment is ready. After 3 months such goods cease to be insured under
   this Section, whether or not the insured continues to hold them.
7. Damage to buttons, buckles, sequins, beads, trims, zips and fastenings, unless the
   garment itself is also damaged.
8. Loss or damage to money, jewellery, documents, keys, spectacles or valuables left in a
   pocket or attached to a garment.
9. Loss or damage caused by moth, vermin or insects, whether present on intake or not.
10. Loss or damage arising from a process the insured was not equipped or competent to
    perform, including the cleaning of a garment the manufacturer marks as not cleanable.
11. Damage caused by the removal of a stain where the customer was told the stain might
    not respond and authorised the attempt.
12. Consequential loss of any kind, including a customer's claim for the cost of hiring a
    replacement garment, travel costs, loss of use, or distress and inconvenience.
13. Any amount recoverable from a subcontractor, to the extent actually recovered.
14. Loss or damage arising from the insured's failure to return a garment where the
    customer has not paid the cleaning charge and the insured is exercising a lien.

### 4.4 Claims conditions specific to this Section

The following conditions are conditions precedent to Meridian's liability under this
Section. They are not administrative preferences. A claim will be assessed against them.

**(a) Intake records.** The insured must create and retain, for every garment taken into
custody, a record showing the date of intake, the customer's name and contact details, a
description of the garment sufficient to identify it, the process requested, the fabric
and care label content where legible, and any pre-existing damage, staining, missing
button or wear noted at the counter. A docket number must be assigned and must be
traceable through the plant to the point of collection.

A description is sufficient if a person who has never seen the garment could pick it from
a rack of similar garments. "Navy wool two-button suit jacket, Zegna label, small ink mark
left cuff" is sufficient. "Suit jacket" is not.

**(b) At-risk authorisations.** Where the insured accepts a garment that is unlabelled,
carries an illegible or implausible label, is trimmed or embellished, is leather or suede,
is a wedding gown, is a vintage or heirloom item, has visible pre-existing damage, or
carries a stain that may not respond, the insured must obtain the customer's authorisation
to proceed at the customer's risk **before** processing. The authorisation must be in
writing or captured electronically, must identify the docket number and the garment, and
must state what the risk is. A verbal authorisation not recorded against the docket is not
an authorisation for the purposes of this condition.

A generic disclaimer printed on the back of the docket is not an at-risk authorisation. It
does not identify the garment, it does not state the risk, and it will not be treated as
one.

**(c) Retention.** Intake records and at-risk authorisations must be retained for not less
than 24 months and must be produced to Meridian on request. Where the insured cannot
produce the intake record for a garment that is the subject of a claim, Meridian may
decline the claim or may settle on the depreciated value of a garment of that general
description, at Meridian's election.

**(d) Reconciliation.** The insured must reconcile garments on the conveyor against open
dockets at least monthly and must record the result, including the number of unmatched
garments and the number of open dockets with no garment against them. A mysterious
disappearance claim will not be considered unless the reconciliation records for the three
months preceding the claim are produced.

**(e) Security and custody.** Garments must be held inside the locked premises outside
trading hours. Garments in the delivery vehicle must not be left in an unattended vehicle
overnight, and the vehicle must be locked when unattended. Garments awaiting collection at
Situation 2 must be behind the counter and not accessible to the public.

**(f) Subcontractors.** Where the insured sends goods to a subcontractor, the insured must
record the consignment, the garments in it, and the date sent and returned. The insured
must not send goods to a subcontractor who has not confirmed in writing that it holds
bailee's cover for goods in its custody.

**(g) Complaint register.** Every customer complaint about the condition of a garment must
be recorded on the day it is made, with the docket number, the complaint, and the action
taken. The register is the first document Meridian asks for when a claim is disputed.

### 4.5 Basis of settlement and disputed value

Settlement is on the basis of the **actual cash value** of the garment at the date of loss,
being the cost of replacing it with a garment of like kind and quality, less a fair
allowance for age, wear and fashion obsolescence. Meridian uses a published garment
depreciation schedule. As a guide:

| Garment | Depreciation | Residual floor |
|---|---|---|
| Wool suit or jacket | 20% per year of age | 20% of replacement cost |
| Business shirt | 33% per year | 10% |
| Formal or bridal gown | 15% per year | 25% |
| Leather or suede garment | 12.5% per year | 20% |
| Overcoat | 15% per year | 20% |
| Household curtains and drapes | 10% per year | 15% |
| Household linen, blankets, doonas | 20% per year | 10% |

**Where the customer's claimed value is disputed.** If the customer asserts a value that
the insured or Meridian does not accept, the following applies:

1. The customer is asked to produce proof of purchase, a bank or card statement, an
   insurance valuation or a retailer's written quotation for a like garment.
2. Where no proof is produced, Meridian will assess replacement cost from the brand, fibre
   content, construction and condition of the garment as recorded on intake, and from
   current retail pricing for a comparable garment.
3. Meridian will not accept a claimed value for a garment described on intake only as, for
   example, "jacket" or "dress", beyond the assessed value of a garment of that general
   description. **The specificity of the intake record is what protects the insured here.**
4. Where the parties remain apart, Meridian may appoint an independent textile assessor at
   Meridian's cost. The assessor's report is not binding on the customer, but Meridian's
   liability is limited to the assessed amount.
5. Meridian will not pay a claim for sentimental value, and will not pay for a garment on
   the basis of its original purchase price where that price is more than five years old,
   except on production of a current valuation.
6. Where a garment is repairable, Meridian will pay the cost of repair rather than
   replacement, provided the repair restores the garment to a saleable condition. Where a
   repair is possible but the customer refuses it, Meridian's liability is limited to the
   cost of the repair.

The insured must not admit liability, agree a value, offer a credit above $250, or settle
a customer complaint that may become a claim, without Meridian's written consent. A
goodwill re-clean, or a refund of the cleaning charge alone, is not an admission and does
not require consent.

### 4.6 How a bailee's claim is handled in practice

1. The customer complains. The complaint is recorded in the register on the day, with the
   docket number and the customer's description of the problem.
2. The garment is retained. It is bagged, tagged with the docket number, and put aside. It
   is not returned to the customer and it is not re-cleaned.
3. The intake docket, the at-risk authorisation if any, and the production record are
   pulled and copied.
4. The claim is notified to the broker within 30 days, with those documents and
   photographs of the garment.
5. Meridian assesses. Where liability and value are clear and the amount is under $5,000,
   Meridian settles on the documents. Otherwise a textile assessor is appointed.
6. Meridian pays the insured, less the $2,500 excess. The insured settles with the
   customer. Because the excess is $2,500, a claim for a single garment worth less than
   that is not worth notifying as a claim, though it should still be recorded in the
   register in case it forms part of a larger event.

### GE1. General exclusions

confiscation, nationalisation or lawful seizure.

### GE2. Wear, tear and gradual causes

This policy does not cover the cost of maintenance, wear and tear, gradual deterioration,
inherent defect, latent defect, or damage that develops over time rather than from an
identified event. This exclusion does not apply to resulting physical damage that is
otherwise insured.

### GE3. Faulty design and workmanship

This policy does not cover the cost of correcting faulty design, faulty materials or
faulty workmanship, but does cover resulting damage to other property.

### GE4. Pollution and contamination — with a dry cleaning carve-back

**Excluded.** This policy does not cover loss, damage, liability, clean-up cost,
remediation cost, fine, penalty or expense of any kind arising directly or indirectly out
of the discharge, dispersal, seepage, migration, release or escape of pollutants or
contaminants, including perchloroethylene, hydrocarbon solvent, solvent residue, separator
water, still bottoms, spent filter cartridges, spent carbon, or any prescribed industrial
waste, into or upon land, the atmosphere, a watercourse, a drain, a sewer or any body of
water.

**Carve-back.** GE4 does **not** exclude loss, damage or liability arising from an escape
of solvent that is **sudden, identifiable, unintended and unexpected**, that commences at
an identifiable time during the period of cover, that is discovered by the insured within
**7 days** of commencing and notified to Meridian in writing within **30 days** of
discovery. Where the carve-back applies, Meridian will pay:

- physical damage to insured property at the situation caused by the escape;
- the cost of cleaning up the escaped solvent within the boundaries of the situation,
  including the removal and lawful disposal of contaminated material through a
  permissioned contractor;
- the cost of emergency response, including the attendance of a hazardous materials
  contractor and the ventilation and testing of the premises;
- the insured's legal liability for damage to third party property or for personal injury
  caused by the escape, within the Section 3 limit; and
- resulting business interruption within the Section 2 limit,

subject to a **sub-limit of $250,000** in the aggregate in any one period of cover, and
the standard excess.

The carve-back does not apply to: an escape that is gradual, repeated or continuing; an
escape from the drainage system, the trade waste connection or a sewer; contamination of
soil or groundwater beneath the situation existing before the period of cover; an escape
while the plant was being operated in breach of a statutory requirement or with a defect
notified in writing by a competent person and unrectified; an escape during the delivery
of solvent by a supplier, which is the supplier's liability; or any cost of remediating
land or groundwater outside the boundaries of the situation.

An example of the carve-back operating: a solvent line to the still fails while the
machine is running, perchloroethylene is released onto the plant floor and into the bund,
the insured discovers it the same day, contains it, engages a permissioned contractor and
notifies Meridian within the week. The clean-up, the damage to the floor covering and to
adjoining stock, and the interruption while the plant is ventilated and tested, are
covered within the sub-limit.

An example of the carve-back not operating: a slow weep from a separator over many months
saturates the slab beneath the machine and is found during a plant upgrade. That is
gradual, it is not discovered within 7 days of commencing, and it is excluded.

### GC12. Definitions

# PART C — MAKING A CLAIM

## C1. What to do first

1. **Make the site safe.** Isolate the machine, shut the gas at the boiler, or secure the
   premises, as the case requires. Do not restart plant that has failed.
2. **Prevent further loss.** Move garments away from water. Cover stock. Board glass.
   Reasonable emergency expense is covered.
3. **Do not throw anything away.** Meridian or its assessor needs to see the damaged
   garment, the failed component or the broken glass. A garment that has been disposed of,
   or a component that has been scrapped by a repairer, will usually be settled at a
   reduced amount or not at all.
4. **Report to the police** where theft, malicious damage or a suspected stolen garment is
   involved, and obtain a report number.
5. **Call the broker on (03) 5442 6180**, or Meridian on 1300 663 004. Claims may be
   lodged 24 hours a day.

## C2. What Meridian needs, by Section

**Property or machinery breakdown (Sections 1 and 5)** — the date, time and circumstances;
photographs of the damage before anything is moved; the make, model, serial number and age
of the plant; a repairer's report identifying the cause; a quotation to repair or replace;
the most recent service record; and for the boiler, the current inspection report and the
daily log book for the period.

**Business interruption (Section 2)** — the last two years of financial statements;
monthly turnover for the 12 months before the damage; the payroll; invoices for outsourced
processing and plant hire; and a written statement of what was done to reduce the loss.

**Liability (Section 3)** — every document received from the claimant, unanswered; the
incident record; the names of witnesses; and photographs of the scene.

**Glass, money, employee dishonesty, tax audit (Sections 6 to 9)** — for glass, a
glazier's quotation and a photograph; for money, the takings record and the banking
record; for employee dishonesty, the reconciliations, the employment record and the police
report; for tax audit, the notice from the authority and the practitioner's fee estimate.

**Bailee's / customers' goods (Section 4)** — see C3.

## C3. The record-keeping that decides a bailee's claim

A Section 4 claim is assessed on the paperwork. Meridian will ask for:

1. **The intake docket** for the garment, showing the date, the customer, the garment
   description, the process requested, the care label content and any pre-existing damage
   noted at the counter.
2. **The at-risk authorisation**, where one was required under 4.4(b).
3. **The production record** showing which machine, which programme, which operator and
   which date the garment was processed on.
4. **The garment itself**, retained and not returned to the customer until Meridian has
   inspected it or agreed in writing that it need not.
5. **The customer's claim**, in writing, stating what they say happened and what they say
   the garment is worth.
6. **Proof of value** — receipt, statement, valuation or retailer's quotation.
7. **The complaint register entry** made on the day the complaint was received.
8. **The reconciliation records** for the three preceding months, where the claim is for a
   garment that cannot be found.
9. **The subcontractor consignment record**, where the garment was sent out.

**In practice, claims fail for four reasons:** the intake record described the garment too
generally to support the value claimed; no at-risk authorisation was obtained for a
garment that plainly needed one; the garment was returned to the customer or disposed of
before it could be inspected; or the loss is a mysterious disappearance with no
reconciliation records behind it. Each of these is within the insured's control at the
counter, and each of them costs nothing to fix.

## C4. Excess

The excess shown against the responding Section is deducted from each claim payment. Where
one event triggers more than one Section, only the highest single excess applies. The
excess is not payable in advance; it is deducted from settlement. Because the bailee's
excess is $2,500, a single-garment claim below that figure is not economic to notify,
though it should still be recorded in the complaint register in case it later forms part
of a larger event or a pattern.

## C5. Assessment and payment

Meridian will acknowledge a claim within 1 business day, appoint an assessor where required
within 3 business days, and make a decision within 10 business days of receiving all
requested information. Where the claim is straightforward and under $5,000, Meridian may
settle on the documents without an assessor. Where the claim is complex, Meridian will
provide an update at least every 20 business days. Payment is made to the insured, not to
the customer, unless the insured directs otherwise in writing.

Progress payments are available on a property claim where reinstatement will take more
than 60 days and the insured has incurred cost. A request for a progress payment should be
made through the broker with supporting invoices.
