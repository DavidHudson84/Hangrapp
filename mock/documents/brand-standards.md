## 1. Who we are

### 1.1 The business

Main Street Dry Cleaners has been cleaning for Bendigo since **1987**. Karen
Whitfield bought it in **March 2016**. There are two sites: the Hargreaves Street
shop, which is also the plant, and the Kangaroo Flat drop store at Lansell
Square. About 1,050 garments a week go through the plant in an ordinary week and
about 1,400 in the September to November peak.

We are the shop with the plant behind it. That is the whole proposition. The
garment does not go on a truck to Melbourne, it goes through the door behind the
counter, and the person who cleaned it is on the premises if there is a question
about it. Almost nothing else we say matters as much as that.

### 1.2 What we stand for

Four things, in this order:

1. **The garment comes back right.** Not fast, not cheap — right. Everything else
   is downstream of this.
2. **We tell you the truth before you leave the counter.** If a stain is unlikely
   to come out, if a fabric is at risk, if we cannot have it by Friday, you hear
   it from us at intake, not at collection.
3. **We are on the premises.** Local owner, local plant, local staff, thirty-nine
   years in the same trade in the same town.
4. **We make it easy.** Two locations, a pickup and delivery run, and a counter
   that remembers you.

### 1.3 Who the customer actually is

- **Bendigo professionals.** Suits, business shirts, dresses. Weekly or
  fortnightly, often the same day each week. Shirts are about 600 a week and this
  is where most of them come from. They want reliability and they do not want to
  think about it.
- **The wedding and formal trade.** Gowns, suits, bridal party, debutante balls,
  race week, school formals. High value, high emotion, low frequency, and the
  single most likely place for something to go wrong in a way that matters. Peak
  is September to November.
- **Four hospitality accounts.** Table linen, napery, front-of-house uniforms.
  Volume, tight turnaround, invoiced monthly. They care about consistency and
  about being able to ring one number.
- **Two aged care contracts.** Resident personal laundry and facility linen.
  Labelled, tracked, returned to the right room. Care and dignity matter here as
  much as cleaning does, and the people who see the result are often families
  rather than the resident.
- **Occasional and one-off.** Doonas, curtains, leather, a coat before winter, a
  jacket before a funeral. Often a first visit. Treat them as though they will be
  a weekly customer, because some of them become one.

### 1.4 What we are deliberately not

- **Not the cheapest.** We do not advertise on price, we do not run "two for one"
  offers, and we do not discount to win work off another cleaner. If a customer's
  only question is price, we answer it honestly and let them decide. Competing on
  price is how a dry cleaner ends up under-cleaning to make the margin, and we do
  not go there.
- **Not a franchise.** No franchisor's palette, no national campaign, no script
  written in another state. Every word a customer reads was written here.
- **Not a laundromat.** We are not self-service and we do not present as one.
- **Not "boutique", "luxury", "premium" or "artisan".** We are a good dry cleaner
  in a country city. The tone is confident and plain, not aspirational.
- **Not apologetic.** We do not open with "we're only a small business". We are a
  thirty-nine-year-old business with a plant on site, and we say so plainly.

## 2. The tagline

**"Bendigo's since 1987"**

### 2.1 What it means

It is a claim of belonging, not a claim of superiority. It says: this business
has been here longer than most of the people reading it have lived here, and it
belongs to the town rather than to a network. The apostrophe is doing the work —
"Bendigo's", the town's own — and it is the reason the line reads as warm rather
than as a boast.

It is deliberately not "Bendigo's best", "Bendigo's finest" or "Bendigo's
favourite". Those are claims we would have to defend and that customers discount
on sight. This one is a fact.

### 2.2 When to use it

- Under the wordmark on shopfront signage, the van, and the window.
- In the footer of letterhead, invoices, quotes and email.
- On the garment bag print.
- On the website in the header and in the About page.
- On the Google Business Profile description.

### 2.3 When not to use it

- **Never in the middle of a sentence.** It is a lockup line, not a phrase. Do not
  write "we've been Bendigo's since 1987 and we'd love to help."
- **Never in a claim letter, a damage response, or any correspondence about
  something that has gone wrong.** Heritage in that context reads as deflection.
- **Never on a price list or a promotional discount piece**, for the same reason
  we do not compete on price.
- **Never with the year changed, updated or "modernised".** It is 1987. It stays
  1987 in 2030.
- Not on internal documents, forms, dockets or staff paperwork.

### 2.4 Typographic treatment

Set in the display serif, sentence case, letter-spaced +40 units at display
sizes, at no more than 45% of the wordmark width, positioned centred under the
wordmark in the stacked lockup or right-aligned to the wordmark baseline in the
horizontal lockup. Always in Brass on dark grounds and in Deep Forest on light
grounds. Never in a third colour, never in italic, never in all caps.

## 3. Logo

### 3.1 What it is

The logo is the **MSDC monogram** — four letters set in the display serif,
letter-spaced and enclosed in a rounded rectangle keyline — with the **hanger
mark** sitting above it. The hanger is a simplified wire coat hanger drawn as a
single continuous stroke: a shoulder line that curves down at each end, and a
neck that rises to a hook turned to the right. It is drawn in one weight, with no
taper, and the hook does not close.

Beneath or beside the monogram sits the **wordmark**: MAIN STREET DRY CLEANERS,
set in the display serif in caps, letter-spaced +80 units.

The mark reads at 30 metres on a shopfront and at 12 mm on a garment tag. That
range is why it is drawn in one weight with no fine detail.

### 3.2 The approved lockups

There are three, and only three.

**Stacked.** Hanger mark above the monogram, wordmark below, tagline below that,
all centred on a common vertical axis. This is the primary lockup. Use it on the
shopfront fascia, the van rear doors, the garment bag, letterhead and the website
header.

**Horizontal.** Hanger mark and monogram at left, wordmark set in two lines at
right, aligned to the cap height of the first line and the baseline of the
second, separated by a 1-unit vertical rule in Brass. Use it where the space is
wider than it is tall: the van side panels, the counter fascia, the window
header, email signatures and the invoice header.

**Mark alone.** Hanger mark and monogram only, no wordmark, no tagline. Use it
only where the name is already unmistakable or where the space is too small for
the wordmark to be legible: hanger tags, the ticket stub, the buttonhole badge,
the favicon, social profile images, and as a repeating pattern on poly print.

### 3.3 Clear space

Clear space on all four sides is equal to **the cap height of the "M" in the
monogram**, measured from the outer edge of the keyline. Nothing enters that
space: no text, no rule, no image edge, no fold, no another logo, no centre
management's tenancy number, no "as seen in" badge.

At sizes below 25 mm wide, increase clear space to 1.5 × cap height, because
small marks crowd more easily than large ones.

### 3.4 Minimum sizes

| Application | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Stacked lockup, print | 32 mm wide |
| Horizontal lockup, print | 45 mm wide |
| Mark alone, print | 12 mm wide |
| Stacked lockup, screen | 120 px wide |
| Horizontal lockup, screen | 180 px wide |
| Mark alone, screen | 40 px wide |
| Embroidery, mark alone | 25 mm wide |
| Etched or engraved | 20 mm wide |

Below these sizes the keyline fills in and the hanger hook closes up. If the
space is smaller than the minimum, use the next simpler lockup rather than
shrinking the current one.

### 3.5 Reversed-out use

The logo reverses to **Bone on Deep Forest** or **Bone on Ink**. In reversed use
the keyline and the hanger mark carry a 0.25 pt weight increase so they hold at
the same optical weight as the positive version — a hairline that is fine on
white disappears on dark.

Brass on Deep Forest is approved for the tagline line only, never for the
monogram or the wordmark, because at signage distance Brass on Deep Forest loses
legibility against a low sun.

The logo may sit on a photograph only where the area behind it is even in tone,
unbusy, and darker than 60% across the whole clear-space area. If it is not, put
the logo on a solid panel instead. There is no approved "scrim", gradient or
drop-shadow treatment for placing the logo over a busy image.

### 3.6 What must never be done to it

1. **Never redraw, retype or rebuild it.** The wordmark is set as outlines. Do not
   re-set it from the font file, however close it looks.
2. **Never stretch, condense or skew it.** Scale proportionally, always.
3. **Never rotate it.** Not on an angle for a sticker, not vertically up a sign,
   not to follow a curve.
4. **Never recolour it** outside the approved palette. No black-and-red version
   for a sale, no pink version for a fundraiser, no team colours in September.
5. **Never add effects** — no drop shadow, bevel, emboss, outer glow, gradient
   fill, or 3D extrude.
6. **Never separate the hanger mark from the monogram** or use the hanger on its
   own as a decorative element, an icon or a bullet.
7. **Never change the spacing** between the mark, the monogram, the wordmark or
   the tagline. The lockups are fixed artwork files.
8. **Never place it inside another shape** — a circle, a badge, a starburst, a
   ribbon, a "since 1987" roundel.
9. **Never crop it or bleed it off an edge**, and never let anything overlap it,
   including a photograph, a price flash or a centre-mandated sticker.

If a supplier says one of these is necessary, the answer is no and the question
comes to Karen.

### 3.7 Artwork files

Master artwork is held in the office in EPS (vector, spot and CMYK), PDF, SVG and
PNG at 300 dpi and at screen sizes, in positive and reversed versions of all
three lockups. Suppliers are sent the file for their process, not asked to
extract one from a website or a PDF. Nadia Farrell keeps the file set and sends
it; nobody emails a logo pulled off the website.

## 4. Colour

### 4.1 The palette

**Deep Forest — primary**
Hex `#1F3D2B` · RGB 31 / 61 / 43 · CMYK 49 / 0 / 29 / 76 · nearest Pantone 3435 C

**Brass — accent**
Hex `#C8A96A` · RGB 200 / 169 / 106 · CMYK 0 / 15 / 47 / 22 · nearest Pantone 4515 C

**Bone — paper**
Hex `#F2EFE7` · RGB 242 / 239 / 231 · CMYK 0 / 1 / 5 / 5 · nearest Pantone 7527 C

**Ink — text**
Hex `#22252A` · RGB 34 / 37 / 42 · CMYK 19 / 12 / 0 / 84 · nearest Pantone 433 C

**Mist — neutral, light**
Hex `#D9D5CB` · RGB 217 / 213 / 203 · CMYK 0 / 2 / 6 / 15 · nearest Pantone Warm Gray 1 C

**Graphite — neutral, mid**
Hex `#5A5E66` · RGB 90 / 94 / 102 · CMYK 12 / 8 / 0 / 60 · nearest Pantone Cool Gray 10 C

Two notes for printers. Bone is normally the **paper**, not an ink — specify an
uncoated natural white stock and leave it unprinted wherever possible; Pantone
7527 C prints noticeably darker than the screen value and is a last resort. And
Deep Forest is a solid, flat colour: it is not built from a rich black and it is
not a duotone.

### 4.2 Proportion

The rough working proportion across any piece is:

- **60% Bone** — paper, walls, garment bags, negative space. The palette is mostly
  the absence of colour.
- **30% Deep Forest** — the fascia, headings, panels, the van body, the uniform.
- **7% Ink and Graphite** — body text, rules, small print, secondary information.
- **3% Brass** — one accent per piece. A rule, a keyline, the tagline, a button. If
  there is Brass in three places on a page, two of them are wrong.

Mist is used to separate — table rows, panel backgrounds, the border of a form —
and never to carry meaning on its own.

Brass is the easiest colour in this palette to ruin the brand with. It reads as
expensive in small quantities and as cheap in large ones. There is no approved
application in which a Brass area is larger than a Deep Forest area on the same
piece.

### 4.3 Accessibility

Contrast ratios, calculated against WCAG 2.1. AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text
and 3:1 for large text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold, and above). AAA requires 7:1.

| Combination | Ratio | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Ink on Bone | 13.4:1 | Pass AAA. The default for body text. |
| Deep Forest on Bone | 10.4:1 | Pass AAA. Headings. |
| Bone on Deep Forest | 10.4:1 | Pass AAA. Reversed panels, the fascia. |
| Bone on Ink | 13.4:1 | Pass AAA. |
| Ink on Mist | 10.5:1 | Pass AAA. Table rows, forms. |
| Bone on Mist | 8.1:1 | Pass AAA — but only as Deep Forest text on Mist; see below. |
| Deep Forest on Mist | 8.1:1 | Pass AAA. |
| Graphite on Bone | 5.7:1 | Pass AA. Small print, captions, secondary text. |
| Brass on Ink | 6.8:1 | Pass AA. Acceptable for short lines. |
| Brass on Deep Forest | 5.3:1 | Pass AA. Approved for the tagline only. |

**Combinations that must never be used for text:**

| Combination | Ratio | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Brass on Bone** | 2.0:1 | Fails everything. This is the most tempting and most common mistake in the whole palette — gold on cream looks lovely and is unreadable. Brass on Bone is permitted as a rule, a keyline or a flat shape, never as type. |
| **Brass on Mist** | 1.5:1 | Fails everything. Never. |
| **Deep Forest on Ink** | 1.3:1 | Fails everything. Two darks together is not a design decision. |
| **Graphite on Mist** | 4.4:1 | Fails AA for normal text; passes for large text only. Do not use it for body copy, form labels or small print. |

On the website, every text colour is checked against its actual background before
it goes live, including text over the header image. "It looked fine on my screen"
is not a check.

## 5. Typography

### 5.1 The typefaces

**Display serif — Libre Caslon Display.** Headings 24 pt and above, the wordmark,
signage, and the tagline. Fallback stack: `"Libre Caslon Display", "Libre Caslon
Text", Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif`.

**Text serif — Libre Caslon Text.** Body copy in printed pieces of more than a
paragraph: letters, the About page, the wedding gown leaflet. Same fallback
stack, without the display cut.

**Sans — Inter.** All screen text, all forms, dockets, tickets, price lists,
labels, and any signage line below the wordmark. Fallback stack: `Inter,
"Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, Arial, system-ui, sans-serif`.

**Mono — IBM Plex Mono.** Ticket numbers, docket numbers, invoice numbers, order
references. Only ever for a reference number, never for prose. Fallback:
`"IBM Plex Mono", "Courier New", monospace`.

All four are open-licence. That is not an accident: it means we can hand the font
files to a signwriter, a printer, an embroiderer and a web developer without a
licence conversation, and every one of them produces the same letterforms. Do not
substitute a "similar" font because a supplier has one already. Send them the
files.

### 5.2 The scale

| Level | Print | Screen | Face | Leading | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display | 40 pt | 44 px | Libre Caslon Display Regular | 1.1 | −10 |
| H1 | 28 pt | 32 px | Libre Caslon Display Regular | 1.2 | −5 |
| H2 | 20 pt | 24 px | Libre Caslon Display Regular | 1.25 | 0 |
| H3 | 15 pt | 19 px | Inter SemiBold | 1.3 | 0 |
| Body | 11 pt | 17 px | Libre Caslon Text / Inter Regular | 1.5 | 0 |
| Small | 9 pt | 14 px | Inter Regular | 1.45 | +5 |
| Caption / legal | 8 pt | 13 px | Inter Regular | 1.4 | +10 |
| Reference number | 11 pt | 16 px | IBM Plex Mono Medium | 1.4 | +20 |

Body text never goes below 9 pt in print or 14 px on screen, including on the
back of a docket and including in terms and conditions. If the terms do not fit,
the terms get shorter or the docket gets bigger. A customer who cannot read the
at-risk clause has not agreed to it in any sense that matters.

Line length in print is 60 to 75 characters. On screen, `max-width: 68ch`.

### 5.3 Signage versus print versus screen

**Signage.** Wordmark in the display serif; every other line in **Inter SemiBold**,
never in the serif. Serifs disappear at distance and in rain. Minimum cap height
on the fascia is 180 mm; on the window, 60 mm; on the van, 90 mm. Letter-spacing
is opened by +20 units on all reversed signage, because light bleeds across dark
grounds at night and closes up the counters.

**Print.** Serif for anything a customer reads sitting down: letters, the gown
leaflet, the About page. Sans for anything read standing up or scanned: price
lists, forms, dockets, labels. Numerals in tables are lining and tabular, never
old-style, so that columns line up.

**Screen.** Sans for everything except H1 and H2. No serif body text on the
website — it is set in Inter at 17 px. Do not use the display serif below 24 px
anywhere on screen; it is drawn for size and falls apart small.

### 5.4 Rules that hold everywhere

- Sentence case for headings. Not Title Case, not ALL CAPS, except the wordmark
  and signage lines.
- One space after a full stop.
- No underlining except a live hyperlink on screen.
- No italic for emphasis in customer-facing material — use bold sparingly instead.
  Italic is for a garment name or a publication, and rarely.
- Never justify text. Ragged right, always.
- No hyphenation in headings or on signage.
- Prices are written `$29.50`, not `$29.50 ea` and not `29.50`. If a price
  includes GST, say so once, plainly.

## 6. Voice

### 6.1 How we write

**Plain.** Short sentences. Ordinary words. A customer standing at a counter with
a child on one hip should be able to read a sign once and understand it.

**Warm.** We know people's names and we write like it. "Morning, Jenny" is
better than "Dear Customer".

**Specific.** "Ready Thursday after 3" beats "ready soon". "The mark on the
right cuff" beats "the stain". Vagueness is where trust goes to die.

**Never apologetic-corporate.** No "we apologise for any inconvenience caused",
no "unfortunately at this time", no "as per our policy", no "we regret to
inform". If something has gone wrong, say what went wrong, say what we will do,
and say it in the first person. "I'm sorry — I got that wrong" is worth more than
three paragraphs of passive voice.

**Never overclaim.** We do not say "guaranteed", "perfect", "like new",
"restored" or "we can get anything out". We say what we will try, what we expect,
and what we cannot promise.

**Never blame the customer.** Not for the stain, not for the delay, not for
losing the ticket. Even when it is true. Especially when it is true.

### 6.2 Say this, not this

| Situation | Say this | Not this |
|---|---|---|
| A job will be late | "It won't be ready Thursday — the shirts are running a day behind. It'll be here Friday from midday. Can I ring you when it's on the rack?" | "Unfortunately due to circumstances beyond our control there has been a delay in processing your order." |
| A stain probably will not come out | "That's a red wine mark that's been through a wash, so the colour's set. I'll work on it, but I'd rather tell you now that it may not come out completely." | "We'll do our best!" |
| A stain definitely will not come out | "I've had a good look at this one and I don't think it's coming out. I can still clean the garment and it'll come back fresh, but the mark will be there. Do you want me to go ahead?" | "Some stains are simply impossible to remove." |
| A customer asks why we cost more than the shop up the road | "We clean everything here, behind that door. Nothing goes on a truck. A two-piece suit is $29.50 and that includes the pressing and any small repairs it needs." | "You get what you pay for." |
| A customer complains about a press | "You're right, that's not our standard. Leave it with me and I'll have it re-pressed today — no charge, obviously." | "It was pressed to our usual specification, however we are happy to review it." |
| A garment is damaged in our care | "This happened here and I'm sorry. I'd like to sort it out properly rather than quickly — can I take some details and ring you tomorrow with what we can do?" | "As per our terms and conditions, liability is limited." |
| A wedding gown enquiry | "Bring it in and we'll go through it together — the fabric, the beading, any marks, and what we can realistically do. Cleaning and boxing starts at $290 and I'll give you a firm price once I've seen it." | "We specialise in luxury bridal couture care." |
| A lost ticket | "No trouble at all. What's the name, and roughly when did you drop it in? We'll find it." | "Garments cannot be released without presentation of the ticket." |
| A customer wants it today | "I can't do today — the machine's already loaded. I can do tomorrow by 11 if you can leave it now." | "Same-day service is subject to availability." |
| Quoting a repair | "Anh can shorten those and re-hem them. It's $34 and she'll need them until Wednesday." | "Alterations are priced on application." |
| Explaining the at-risk form | "This is a lovely old jacket and the lining's gone brittle — that happens with age and there's nothing anyone can do about it. Cleaning it might make it worse. If you're happy for me to try, I'll ask you to sign here so we've both said the same thing out loud." | "Please sign this disclaimer waiving our liability." |
| Answering the phone | "Main Street Dry Cleaners, Sharon speaking." | "Good morning and thank you for calling Main Street Dry Cleaners, this is Sharon, how may I direct your call today?" |
| Closing a conversation | "See you Friday." | "Thank you for choosing Main Street Dry Cleaners. We appreciate your business." |
| A price rise | "Our prices went up on 1 July — a suit's $29.50 now, up from $28. First rise in two years." | "Due to increased operational costs we have been forced to implement a modest price adjustment." |

### 6.3 Words we do not use

*Luxury. Premium. Bespoke. Artisan. Curated. Journey. Experience. Solutions.
Utilise. Commence. Prior to. In order to. Reach out. Touch base. At this point in
time. As per. Please be advised. We apologise for any inconvenience.*

If a sentence contains one of these, it can be rewritten shorter and it will be
better.

## 7. Applications

### 7.1 Shopfront signage — 118 Hargreaves Street, Bendigo

- **Fascia:** flat aluminium panel, 4,200 mm × 700 mm, painted Deep Forest, with
  the horizontal lockup in Bone applied as cut vinyl. Wordmark cap height 180 mm.
  Tagline in Brass, cap height 55 mm, right-aligned to the wordmark.
- **Illumination:** LED trough uplight along the fascia base, 3000 K warm white,
  on a timer from dusk to 22:00. No internally illuminated box, no neon, no
  flashing, no colour-change.
- **Under-awning blade:** double-sided, 600 mm × 400 mm, Deep Forest ground, mark
  alone in Bone, projecting so that it is visible from the Bath Lane end of the
  footpath.
- **Door:** street number and trading hours in Inter SemiBold, Bone on Deep
  Forest, 25 mm cap height, at eye level. Hours are corrected the day they change,
  and public holiday hours go up seven days ahead.
- Landlord's written consent under the lease is obtained before any change to the
  fascia, the blade or the illumination, and a planning check is made first under
  the environmental manual's clause on Council obligations.

### 7.2 Shopfront signage — Shop 12, Lansell Square, Kangaroo Flat

The centre's design guidelines set the fascia size and the illumination type, and
Aurora Retail Management approves the artwork before it is made. Within those
limits:

- Fascia in Deep Forest with the horizontal lockup in Bone, wordmark cap height as
  large as the guidelines permit, minimum 120 mm.
- **"Drop store"** is never used on the sign. The line beneath the wordmark reads
  **"Drop off and collect"** in Inter SemiBold, Brass, 45 mm cap height, because
  "drop store" is our word and not the customer's.
- No centre-supplied generic signage, no promotional overlays, no third-party
  stickers on the glass.
- The counter carries a card explaining that garments are cleaned at the Bendigo
  plant and giving the Bendigo phone number, because customers ask and Rebecca
  should not have to explain it twice a day.

### 7.3 Window vinyl

- Frosted band across the lower 900 mm of the shop glass, with the mark alone
  reversed out of the frost, repeating at 1,400 mm centres. It gives privacy at
  the counter and it stops people walking into the glass.
- The service list — Dry cleaning · Shirt laundry · Alterations · Wedding gowns ·
  Curtains and household · Pickup and delivery — in Inter SemiBold, Deep Forest,
  35 mm cap height, single column, left-aligned, at eye level.
- Maximum 25% of the glazed area covered, so the shop reads as open.
- **Never** in the window: price flashes, "SALE", starbursts, handwritten signs,
  A4 pages sticky-taped up, faded posters, centre promotions, or anything printed
  on office paper. If a message is worth putting in the window it is worth
  printing properly. If it is temporary, it goes on the counter card holder, not
  the glass.

### 7.4 The counter

The counter is the brand. More customers form their impression of us there than
anywhere else, including the website.

- Bench in solid timber with a Deep Forest painted front panel and a Brass rule at
  the top edge of the panel.
- Horizontal lockup, mark alone at 200 mm, centred on the front panel.
- Behind the counter: the conveyor visible through glazing. We want people to see
  the plant. It is our best argument.
- On the counter: the ticket printer, one A5 card holder, a bowl of spare buttons,
  and nothing else. No lolly jar, no charity tin, no supplier point-of-sale, no
  clutter.
- The A5 card holder carries one card at a time. Approved cards: pickup and
  delivery, wedding gown care, alterations, and seasonal opening hours.

### 7.5 Garment bags and poly print

- Standard poly is clear, with the **mark alone printed in Deep Forest** at 90 mm,
  repeating at 600 mm centres down the length of the roll, plus one line of small
  print at the bottom of each bag: `Main Street Dry Cleaners · Bendigo's since
  1987 · (03) 5443 8817`.
- The bag is a billboard that walks through the CBD and hangs in a wardrobe for a
  year. It is worth printing well and it is not worth crowding.
- **Wedding gown and formal:** heavyweight breathable cotton garment bag, Bone,
  with the stacked lockup embroidered in Deep Forest at 140 mm on the front panel
  and a Brass zip pull. These are not disposable and are charged for within the
  gown price.
- **Aged care and hospitality accounts:** unprinted heavy-duty poly, because those
  garments travel in bulk and printed poly reads as retail packaging in a
  commercial delivery.
- Never print a phone number, a website and a social handle on the same bag. One
  contact route, which is the phone.

### 7.6 Hangers

- Standard: black plastic contour hanger with a Brass-finish hook.
- Shirts: white plastic, no hook colour, boxed collar support.
- Formal and gowns: padded satin hanger, Bone, with a woven label carrying the
  mark alone.
- Hanger tags are Bone card, 40 mm × 90 mm, mark alone at 12 mm at the top, ticket
  number in IBM Plex Mono at 11 pt, and the garment description in Inter at 9 pt.
- Wire hangers are not used for customer returns. They come back to us for

### 7.7 Tickets and dockets

## 8. The customer-facing documents

Four documents do most of the damage or most of the good: the quote, the invoice,
the claim letter and the at-risk authorisation. They are the only pieces of
writing a customer keeps.

### 8.1 The quote

Used for wedding gowns, curtains and household, restoration work, and any
commercial account.

- Letterhead. Addressed to a person by name.
- One paragraph saying what we looked at and what we found, in plain words: "I've
  had a look at the gown. The silk is in good order. There's a grey mark on the
  hem about 300 mm long, and some scuffing to the underside of the train."
- What we propose to do, as a short list.
- The price, once, in bold, with GST stated. If it is a range, say why it is a
  range and what would move it.
- How long it will take, as a date.
- What we cannot promise, said plainly and without hedging.
- Valid for 30 days. Signed by name — Karen or Sharon, never "The Team".

### 8.2 The invoice

- Horizontal lockup, invoice number in IBM Plex Mono, entity name and ABN, date,
  and payment terms in one block at the top right.
- Item lines in Inter at 10 pt, tabular numerals, one line per garment or per
  batch. A customer should be able to match every line to something they handed
  over.
- Total in bold, GST shown separately, payment methods listed with the bank
  details in full.
- Commercial accounts are invoiced monthly in arrears, 14 days.
- Overdue notices are courteous and short and are sent by Nadia. A first reminder
  is a reminder, not a demand. Nothing about a debt is ever discussed at the
  counter within earshot of other customers.

### 8.3 The claim letter

When a garment has been lost or damaged in our care.

- Always on letterhead, always posted or emailed as a PDF, never as a text message
  and never handed over the counter to be read on the spot.
- Opens with what happened and that it happened here. No throat-clearing, no
  history of the business, no tagline.
- Says what we are offering and why, in figures.
- States the relevant limit if one applies — our bailee's liability cover carries a
  $25,000 inner limit per garment within a $50,000 limit for any one event — but
  never leads with it, and never uses it as an argument for paying less than is
  fair.
- Gives a name and a direct phone number to reply to.
- Signed by Karen or by Sharon, in full, with a role.
- Never uses: "as per our terms", "we deny liability", "without prejudice"
  (unless a broker has advised it), "goodwill gesture" (it is either owed or it is
  a gift; say which), or the tagline.

**The rule.** Anything going to a customer about a claim — a lost garment, a
damaged garment, a dispute over money, an insurance matter — is **written by Karen
Whitfield or Sharon Delaney and by nobody else.** Not by counter staff, not by the
office, not by a supplier, not by a template. Anyone else who is asked for one
says "I'll have Karen ring you today" and then makes sure Karen knows. Every
claim letter is copied to Hargreaves & Locke where the matter may become an
insurance claim, and a copy is filed with the incident record.

### 8.4 The at-risk authorisation

- A5, Bone card, two-part carbonless — customer keeps one, we file one.
- Header: mark alone, "At-risk cleaning authorisation", and the authorisation
  number in IBM Plex Mono.
- Fields: date; customer name and phone; ticket number; garment description; the
  specific risk, written in the staff member's own words in the space provided,
  not ticked from a list; what we propose to do; what may happen; customer
  signature; staff signature.
- Body text at 10 pt minimum. The risk statement is handwritten because a
  handwritten sentence proves a conversation happened and a tick box does not.
- Never described to a customer as a "waiver" or a "disclaimer". It is an
  agreement about what we both expect.
- Filed for 7 years with the ticket record.

### 9.3 Review responses

Every review gets a response. Positive reviews get two sentences and a name.
Negative reviews get a response written by Karen or Sharon, never by anyone else,
and never on the same day it lands.

The rules for a negative response:

1. Thank them, by name if the name is real.
2. Acknowledge the specific thing. Do not restate it defensively.
3. Do not argue, do not correct their version in public, and do not mention
   anything they got wrong.
4. Never disclose anything about their garment, their account or their visit. A
   public reply that reveals what somebody brought in is a worse problem than the
   review.
5. Move it offline with a name and a direct number.
6. Keep it under 80 words.
7. If it concerns a live claim, the response says only that we are in contact and
   nothing else. Karen clears it with the broker first.

**Worked example — one star, "They ruined my husband's jacket and won't do
anything about it."**

> Thank you for taking the time to write, Meredith. I'm sorry — a damaged garment
> is exactly the thing we most want to get right, and it sounds like we haven't.
> I'd like to look at it properly with you rather than sort it out through a
> review. I'm at the shop most days and my direct line is (03) 5443 8817. Please
> ask for me.
>
> Karen Whitfield, owner

Note what it does not do. It does not say what the garment was, what the fabric
was, what we think happened, what the customer was told at intake, or what our
terms say. It does not use "unfortunately", it does not use the tagline, and it
does not defend us. It takes the conversation somewhere it can actually be
resolved. If the matter is with the insurer, the last line changes to "We're
working through this with our insurer and I'll keep you updated directly."

## 10. Photography

### 10.1 Style

Natural light. Real premises, real people, real garments. Shot on the shop floor
and in the plant, not in a studio and not bought from a library. Slight warmth in
the grade, no heavy filters, no black-and-white "heritage" treatment, no tilted
horizons, no shallow-focus close-ups of a single button.

Composition is level, unhurried and uncluttered. Deep Forest, Bone and steel are
the colours that appear naturally in our premises and photographs should look
like they belong to the same palette as everything else here.

### 10.2 What to shoot

- The shopfront, in the morning, with the light on the fascia.
- The counter with someone actually behind it.
- The conveyor loaded — it is the most legible image of what we do.
- Hands at work: pressing, spotting, hand-finishing a collar, pinning an
  alteration.
- The plant, tidy, in operation, with everyone in the correct protective
  equipment.
- Finished garments on the rack, bagged, in rows.
- Detail: a hanger tag, a stack of shirt boxes, a row of buttons.

### 10.3 What never to shoot

- A customer's garment that is identifiable, without that customer's written
  permission.
- Any customer's face, name, ticket, docket or account without permission.
- The counter screen, a docket in close-up, or anything showing a name, address or
  phone number.
- Anyone working without the correct protective equipment, and anything that would
  read as a solvent handling breach — an open machine door, an open drum, a
  transfer in progress.
- The solvent store, the waste bay, or the waste containers. Not because there is
  anything to hide, but because a photograph of a labelled hazardous waste drum
  ends up in a context nobody intended.
- An untidy plant, an overflowing rack, or a bench covered in someone's lunch.
- Anything at all connected with a garment under complaint or claim.

Photographs of staff require their consent, which is recorded, and which they may
withdraw at any time. When someone leaves, their photographs come down within a
month.

## 11. The counter script

Not a script to be recited — a shape for the conversation, so that every customer
gets the same standard of attention whether they are met by Sharon, Emma,
Rebecca or Chloe on a Saturday.

### 11.1 The greeting

Look up. Say hello before they reach the counter. If you know them, use their
name.

> "Morning. What have you got for me?"

If there is a queue, acknowledge the person waiting — "I'll be right with you" —
because being seen is most of what waiting requires.

### 11.2 The intake conversation

Handle the garment. Do not take it and turn away.

> "Right — let's have a look at this one."

Then, in order: **inspect, ask, tell, promise.**

**Inspect.** Open it out on the counter. Check collar, cuffs, underarms, hem,
lining, pockets. Empty the pockets in front of them and hand back what is in
them. Look for existing damage before it becomes ours.

**Ask.** "Anything on it I should know about?" — then the two questions that
actually matter: "What is it?" and "How long has it been there?" A fresh spill
and a six-month-old mark are different problems and the customer is the only one
who knows which it is.

**Tell.** Say what you found, including what they did not mention.

> "There's a mark on the right cuff here, and the lining's come away a little at
> the left shoulder. I'll get Anh to look at the shoulder while it's here — that's
> about $12."

**Promise.** A date and a time.

> "It'll be ready Thursday from 3."

Never promise a plant turnaround you have not checked, and never promise
same-day at the counter without asking Peter.

### 11.3 Raising the at-risk conversation

The aim is to be honest without frightening anybody. Frame it as the garment's
condition, not as our reluctance, and offer a decision rather than a warning.

> "Can I show you something? This lining's gone brittle — see how it's splitting
> along the fold? That's age, it happens to every jacket of this vintage, and
> there's nothing anyone can do to stop it. What it means is that cleaning could
> make it worse rather than better.
>
> I'm happy to go ahead if you'd like me to, and I'd take it steady. But I'd
> rather tell you now than ring you Thursday. If you want me to try, I'll ask you
> to sign this so we've both said the same thing out loud."

Three rules. Show them the problem rather than describing it. Give them the
decision — never make it for them. And never use the words "waiver",
"disclaimer", "liability" or "at your own risk" at the counter; they turn a
conversation into a transaction.

If the customer would rather not, that is a good outcome. "Then let's leave it —
you can always bring it back."

### 11.4 The collection

> "Hello — have you got your ticket? No trouble if not, what's the name?"

Bring the garment out and **show it to them before it goes in the bag.** Turn it
so they can see the part that was marked.

> "That cuff came up well. The shoulder's been re-stitched — you can just see
> where, but it'll hold."

If there is anything they should know — a mark that has not fully lifted, a repair
that is visible, a shortened hem — say it now, at the counter, while the garment is
in front of them. Never let a customer find it at home.

### 11.5 When a garment has gone wrong

Use this phrase. It is short and it is honest and it does not commit anyone to
anything before Karen has looked at it:

> **"This happened here and I'm sorry. I'd like to sort it out properly rather
> than quickly. Can I take your details and have Karen ring you tomorrow?"**

Then: write it down while they are standing there, give them a time, and make
sure Karen actually knows before you go home. Do not offer a figure, do not offer
a refund, do not mention insurance, and do not explain what you think went wrong.
Do not say "these things happen". They do, but not to the person whose jacket it
was.

If a customer is angry, take it away from the counter and away from the queue.
Nobody is expected to absorb abuse; if it goes that way, say "I'm going to get
Sharon" and go and get Sharon.
