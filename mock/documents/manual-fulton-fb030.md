## 1. DESCRIPTION

The FB-030 is a vertical, tubeless, forced-draught, gas-fired steam boiler. Two
concentric cylindrical shells joined at top and bottom form an annular water
space around a central cylindrical furnace. The burner fires downward into the
furnace from the top head. Combustion gases pass down the furnace, turn at the
base and rise through a spirally wound heat exchange passage between the furnace
shell and the outer shell before discharging to the flue connection at the upper
rear.

There are no tubes. The only pressure boundary penetrations are the steam
outlet, the feedwater inlet, the safety valve connection, the two blowdown
connections, the gauge glass connections, the level probe pocket, the pressure
control tapping and two handholes. Steam is drawn from the upper annulus through
a perforated dry pipe and is nominally 98% dry at rated output with boiler water
within the limits at Section 6.

The boiler is supplied as a package on a fabricated steel base with the burner,
gas train, feedwater pump, control panel, level controls, safety valve, gauge
glass and blowdown valves mounted and wired.

**Design intent.** The FB-030 is a low-water-content boiler. It raises steam
quickly — cold to 620 kPa in about 12 minutes — and is correspondingly
intolerant of a loss of feedwater. Water content at normal working level is
415 litres. At full output it evaporates 470 kg/h, or 7.8 kg per minute. **A
total feedwater failure at full fire takes the boiler from normal working level
to first low water in under six minutes.** Sections 6, 7 and 8 follow from that
number.

## 2. SAFETY

### 2.1 The two things that will kill you

**Stored energy.** At 690 kPa the water in the boiler is at approximately
170 °C and is liquid only because it is under pressure. If the pressure boundary
fails — a cracked shell, a blown gasket, an inspection cover opened under
pressure — a large fraction of that water flashes instantly to steam and expands
roughly 1,600 times. The 415 litres in an FB-030 at working level contains
enough energy to destroy the plant room.

**Scalding.** Steam at 170 °C, condensate at 100 °C and boiler water blown down
at working temperature all cause full-thickness burns in under a second. A steam
leak under pressure is frequently invisible for the first 100–200 mm. Never
search for a steam leak with your hand — use a broom handle held at arm's length
and watch for the split in the timber.

### 2.2 Low water

**DANGER.** Firing with the water level below the lowest permissible water level
overheats the furnace shell. Steel loses strength progressively above about
425 °C. The failure is either a bulge developing over minutes, or — if water
then contacts the overheated surface — a rapid steam generation and a rupture.

The lowest permissible water level (LWL) is stamped on a plate beside the gauge
glass, 165 mm above the furnace crown. The first low water probe operates 25 mm
above LWL; the second operates at LWL.

**If the gauge glass shows no water:**

1. Do not add water. Do not open the feedwater valve or start the feed pump.
2. Press the emergency stop.
3. Close the manual gas isolation valve at the boiler.
4. Leave the boiler alone. Do not open blowdown valves or any inspection opening.
5. Call the service contractor. Record the event in the log book.

Adding cold water to a boiler that has been fired dry is the most reliable way
to rupture it. The boiler must cool naturally — six to eight hours for an
FB-030 — and be internally inspected before it is refilled.

### 2.3 Overpressure

The safety relief valve is the last line of defence and the only device on the
boiler needing no power, no signal and no logic. It is set at 690 kPa and
sealed.

**DANGER.** Never gag, wire down, weight, adjust or break the seal on the safety
relief valve. Never fit an isolating valve between the boiler and the valve.
Never fit a valve, restriction, plug or trap in the discharge line. The
discharge line must be independently supported so its weight does not bear on
the valve body, and must terminate outside the building where a sudden full-flow
discharge of 520 kg/h of steam cannot reach a person, walkway, doorway or window.

If the valve lifts in service, shut down, find and correct the cause, and have
the valve inspected for seat damage before returning to service. A valve that
has lifted and failed to reseat cleanly must be overhauled or replaced by a
competent person. **RECORD** every lift with the date, the lifting pressure and
the reseating pressure.

### 2.4 Gas

The FB-030 burns natural gas at 15 kPa nominal inlet and 363 kW full fire input.
The installation downstream of the meter is subject to AS 5601 and the Gas
Safety Act 1997 (Vic).

**WARNING.** At 363 kW input this is a **Type B appliance**. Gas work —
including replacing a solenoid, regulator, pressure switch or any gas train
component — must be done only by a person holding a current Victorian gas
fitting licence with the appropriate Type B endorsement. An operator must not
open the gas train.

If you smell gas: do not operate any electrical switch, including the light
switch and the emergency stop; close the manual gas isolation valve if it can be
reached without passing the suspected leak; evacuate and ventilate by opening
external doors; call the gas supplier's emergency number from outside; do not
re-enter until cleared.

### 2.5 Carbon monoxide

Incomplete combustion produces carbon monoxide — colourless, odourless, and
above about 400 ppm fatal to an adult within two hours. Headache, nausea and
drowsiness are easily mistaken for a long shift in a hot plant room.

The causes are all foreseeable in a dry cleaning plant: a blocked or restricted
flue; a corroded flue joint discharging into the room; insufficient combustion
air; a burner out of adjustment; and negative pressure in the plant room created
by extraction fans elsewhere in the building drawing the flue in reverse.

**STATUTORY.** Combustion is to be analysed and recorded at every service. CO in
the flue gas, air-free, must not exceed 100 ppm. Above 100 ppm the burner must
be adjusted before return to service; above 400 ppm the boiler must be shut down
and the flue and air supply investigated. A CO monitor with an audible alarm is
to be fitted at head height within 3 m of the boiler and function-tested monthly.

### 2.6 Confined space entry for internal inspection

The water space and the furnace are confined spaces within the meaning of
AS 2865.

**STATUTORY.** Internal inspection or internal repair requires a confined space
entry permit, trained persons, atmospheric testing before and during entry,
continuous ventilation, a standby person at the opening, and rescue
arrangements in place before entry begins.

Before any opening is broken:

1. Boiler shut down, isolated, at atmospheric pressure — confirmed by an open
   vent and a gauge reading zero.
2. Drained and cooled below 30 °C.
3. Gas isolated at the manual valve and locked out.
4. Electrical supply isolated at the isolator and locked out.
5. Steam, feedwater, condensate and blowdown lines isolated by physical
   disconnection or double block and bleed. **A single closed valve is not
   isolation.** A common blowdown vessel or a common steam header can back-feed
   steam or hot water into a vessel someone is standing in.
6. Atmosphere tested for oxygen (19.5–23.5%), carbon monoxide and residual
   chemical vapour from lay-up.

A boiler laid up wet on sulphite chemistry can have an oxygen-depleted
atmosphere in the water space — sodium sulphite scavenges the air as readily as
the water. **This has killed people. Test before entry, every time.**

### 2.9 What an operator may and may not do

**An operator MAY:** start and stop the boiler on the normal controls; read and
record pressure, water level, feedwater temperature, hotwell level, chemical
drum levels and running hours; blow down the gauge glass and confirm the level
returns; carry out bottom and surface blowdown to 8.6; carry out the daily and
weekly proof tests at 7.10; test boiler water and feedwater and adjust dosing
within the range set by the treatment supplier; refill the brine tank and
initiate a manual regeneration; clean the gauge glass externally; top up the
hotwell; reset a non-safety lockout once in accordance with 10.3; and shut the
boiler down and isolate it in an emergency.

**An operator MAY NOT:**

- Reset any lockout arising from a safety device more than once, or at all if
  the cause is not understood.
- Adjust, bypass, disconnect, jumper or temporarily defeat any low water
  device, pressure switch, gas pressure switch, air proving switch, flame
  detector or valve proving system.
- Adjust the operating setpoint above 620 kPa or the high limit above 655 kPa.
- Adjust, seal-break or dismantle the safety relief valve.
- Open, adjust, repair or replace any part of the gas train.
- Open any handhole, inspection opening or the burner mounting.
- Change the burner air setting, gas ratio or any combustion adjustment.
- Enter the boiler.
- Carry out any work inside the control panel.
- Add water to a boiler that has been fired low or dry.
- Operate the boiler with a known defective safety device, for any period, for
  any reason, including to finish a shift's work.

### 2.10 The legal position of an unattended boiler

AS 2593 defines three attendance categories: **continuously attended**, where a
person is in the boiler house at all times; **limited attendance**, where the
boiler may operate without a person in attendance for a defined maximum period
subject to specified safeguards, testing and records; and **unattended**, which
requires a higher standard of controls, water treatment and monitoring again.

**The FB-030 as supplied is certified for limited attendance operation with a
maximum unattended period of 24 hours**, subject to all of the following being
true at all times:

1. Two independent low water devices are fitted and functional, one of them
   manual reset and independent of the level control.
2. Both are proof-tested at the frequency at 7.10 and the test is recorded.
3. Automatic water treatment is in service and feedwater is within Section 6.
4. Flame supervision has a flame failure response time not exceeding 1 second,
   with a proven pre-purge and safe shutdown on any safety interlock.
5. A competent person is appointed with responsibility for the boiler and the
   daily checks are carried out and recorded.
6. The boiler room is not used for storage of combustibles and is not
   accessible to untrained persons.

**"Limited attendance" does not mean "leave it running and go home."** It means
the boiler may run through a period when nobody is standing beside it, provided
a trained person is on the premises, the boiler is checked at the required
interval, and the safeguards above are proven. At this installation the boiler
is fired only during production hours and an operator is on site whenever the
burner is enabled.

If any one of the six conditions is not satisfied the boiler reverts to
continuously attended operation and may be fired only with a trained operator in
the boiler room. If the operator leaves the room, the burner is switched off.

**STATUTORY.** Operating a boiler unattended when a safety device required for
unattended operation is defective, bypassed or overdue for proof testing is a
breach of the operator's duty under the Occupational Health and Safety
Regulations 2017 (Vic), invalidates the basis on which the plant is registered,
and voids both the insurance and the manufacturer's warranty (Section 12).

## 3. STATUTORY AND COMPLIANCE

### 3.1 Standards referenced

**AS 1210 — Pressure vessels.** The design and construction standard: design
rules, materials, welding qualification, NDE, hydrostatic test and nameplate
marking. The FB-030 is built to AS 1210 Class 2H. For the operator it is mostly
historical — it governs what was built — but it becomes live the moment a repair
to a pressure part is proposed, because that repair must be to the original
construction standard.

**AS 2593 — Boilers: safety management and supervision systems.** The
operational standard: attendance categories (2.10), the safety devices required
for each, the proof testing regime, competency of attendants, and the records to
be kept. This is the standard that determines whether the boiler may lawfully be
left running while nobody is beside it.

**AS 3788 — Pressure equipment: in-service inspection.** The inspection
standard: inspection types, maximum intervals, competency of the inspector,
reporting and record retention. It is the standard the inspection body works to
and against which the plant registration is maintained.

**AS/NZS 3000 — Electrical installations.** The supply, isolator, earthing and
equipment requirements for the boiler room.

**AS 5601 — Gas installations.** The gas installation from the meter to the
appliance, the gas train, ventilation for combustion air, flue requirements, and
certification of Type B appliances.

**AS 4343 — Pressure equipment: hazard levels.** Determines the hazard level and
therefore whether the design and the item require registration. The FB-030 is
hazard level B.

**AS 2865 — Confined spaces.** Applies to internal inspection and repair.

**AS 3814 — Industrial and commercial gas-fired appliances.** The burner, the
gas train sequence, the valve proving system and the flame safeguard.

### 3.2 Plant registration and design registration — the difference

These are two registrations, held by two different parties. Confusing them is
the most common compliance failure found at audit.

**Design registration** registers the *drawing*, not any particular boiler. The
designer submits the design with calculations, a design verifier confirms
compliance with AS 1210, and the regulator issues a number. Every FB-030 built
to that design shares it, and it is recorded on the nameplate. For this model it
is **VIC-DR-PV-11-0592**. The operator does not hold it, does not renew it and
cannot lose it.

**Plant registration** — also called item registration — registers *this
individual boiler, at this address, under this operator*. It is held by the
person with management or control of the plant, here **Aveline Holdings Pty
Ltd**, and the number is **VIC-PV-118-4470**. It is issued by WorkSafe Victoria
under the Occupational Health and Safety Regulations 2017 (Vic) Part 3.5 on the
basis of a satisfactory inspection by a competent person, evidence of design
registration, and the plant records.

The plant registration:

- is specific to this boiler and to this address;
- does not transfer automatically if the boiler is moved, even between rooms in
  the same building, where the move changes the installation;
- must be renewed — the current certificate for VIC-PV-118-4470 expires on
  **30 June 2027**;
- is conditional on inspections continuing at the required intervals and on the
  reports being satisfactory;
- may be suspended or cancelled if the boiler is found operating with a
  defective safety device or an overdue inspection.

**STATUTORY.** Operating a registrable item of plant that is not registered, or
whose registration has lapsed, is an offence. Diarise the expiry at least 90
days ahead so the annual inspection, any outstanding items and the renewal
paperwork can be completed in time.

### 3.3 Inspection types and intervals

These are maximum intervals. The inspection body may shorten any of them on the
basis of what it finds, and a shortened interval becomes the applicable one.

| Inspection | Maximum interval | Carried out by |
|---|---|---|
| External inspection, in service | 12 months | Competent person (AS 3788), independent of the operator |
| Internal inspection, water and fire side | 24 months | Competent person (AS 3788) |
| Hydrostatic test at 1,035 kPa | 72 months, or after any pressure part repair or relocation | Competent person, witnessed |
| Safety relief valve overhaul, test and reset | 24 months | Accredited valve service organisation |
| Combustion analysis and burner service | 6 months | Licensed Type B gasfitter |
| Low water device internal examination, probes withdrawn | 12 months | Service technician |
| Full control function test to AS 2593 | 12 months | Service technician, witnessed by the operator |
| Gas train integrity and valve proving verification | 12 months | Licensed Type B gasfitter |

The **external inspection** covers the shell and heads for distortion,
corrosion and leakage, the insulation, the mountings, the safety valve and its
discharge, the gauge glass, the level controls, the blowdown arrangement, the
flue, the gas train, the controls and the records.

The **internal inspection** requires shutdown, cooling, draining, opening at the
handholes and removal of the burner. The water side is examined for scale,
sludge, pitting, grooving at the furnace-to-shell weld and evidence of
overheating; the fire side for soot, corrosion, refractory condition and
distortion. It is a confined space entry — see 2.6.

The **hydrostatic test** pressurises the vessel with water to 1,035 kPa, being
1.5 × MAWP, and holds it while the vessel is examined. The safety valve is
removed and the connection blanked for the test, and refitted afterwards.

### 3.4 Who may carry out inspections

**STATUTORY.** In-service inspections under AS 3788 must be carried out by a
**competent person** — one with the qualifications, training and experience to
inspect pressure equipment of this type, who is independent of the operator. In
practice this means an accredited inspection body; here, Goldfields Plant
Inspection Services Pty Ltd.

The operator cannot inspect their own boiler for AS 3788 purposes. Neither can
the service technician who maintains it, because the independence requirement is
not satisfied. Servicing and inspection are separate functions producing
separate reports.

Boiler operation at this hazard level does not require a licence in Victoria,
but it does require **demonstrated and documented competency**. AS 2593 expects
the person attending the boiler to understand the level controls, the
significance of low water, the blowdown procedure, and the action to be taken on
each alarm and lockout condition.

inspector, an insurer or a regulator asks for after an incident.

**RECORD.** The log book is kept in the plant room, not the office. An entry is
made at each start-up and each shift, in ink, not erased; errors are struck
through with a single line and initialled.

### 5.3 Ventilation and combustion air

Combustion air at full fire is 425 m³/h. Ventilation must supply that plus air

- Gas input 363 kW.
- Low level (combustion air) free area at 300 mm² per kW: 363 × 300 =
  108,900 mm² = 0.109 m².
- High level (relief) free area at 150 mm² per kW: 363 × 150 = 54,450 mm² =
  0.055 m².
- Subtotal 0.164 m², plus 0.021 m² for the two dryers vented to the same space

Free area is the net open area of the grille, not the size of the hole in the
wall. A typical louvre has a free area of 50–60% of its face area, so a 0.109 m²
free area needs a louvre of about 0.20 m² face area. Low level openings within
300 mm of floor level, high level within 300 mm of ceiling level, both
communicating directly with outside air.

### 5.9 Condensate return

Return as much condensate as is practical: every litre returned is hot, soft,

and the shirt unit return well, while steam injected directly into a process is
lost.

## 6. WATER TREATMENT

### 6.1 Why this section matters

More boilers are destroyed by water than by fire. Scale, corrosion, caustic
attack and carryover between them account for the great majority of pressure
part failures, and every one of them is preventable by controlling the water.
Water treatment is also the cheapest thing in the plant room: the chemicals, the
salt and the test kit together cost a fraction of what one millimetre of scale
costs in gas over a year (6.7).

### 6.2 Feedwater limits and why each matters

Feedwater is softened make-up plus returned condensate, after dosing, measured
at the hotwell or the pump suction.

| Parameter | Limit | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Total hardness as CaCO₃ | ≤ 2 mg/L, target < 1 | Calcium and magnesium precipitate onto the hottest surface and form scale. Nothing else on this list damages the boiler faster. |
| Total dissolved solids | ≤ 200 mg/L | Everything dissolved in the feedwater concentrates in the boiler. High feed TDS forces more blowdown, wasting heat and water. |
| pH at 25 °C | 8.5–9.5 | Below 8.5 the hotwell, pump and feed line corrode. Above 9.5 excess alkalinity is carried forward into the boiler. |
| Total alkalinity as CaCO₃ | ≤ 120 mg/L | Bicarbonate alkalinity breaks down in the boiler to carbon dioxide, which dissolves in the condensate as carbonic acid and eats the return lines from the inside. |
| Dissolved oxygen | ≤ 0.05 mg/L | Oxygen causes pitting corrosion. Pitting is localised, deep and fast, and perforates a shell long before general corrosion would. |
| Silica as SiO₂ | ≤ 5 mg/L | Forms a hard glassy scale not removed by ordinary acid cleaning, and volatilises into the steam at pressure. |
| Total iron as Fe | ≤ 0.1 mg/L | Indicates corrosion upstream, and deposits as iron oxide on heat transfer surfaces. |
| Suspended solids | ≤ 5 mg/L | Settles as sludge, insulates, and blocks the blowdown line. |
| Oil and grease | ≤ 1 mg/L | Causes violent foaming and carryover and forms an insulating film on the furnace. One press failure can put enough oil into the condensate to matter. |
| Temperature | 82–90 °C | Hot feedwater has already released most of its dissolved oxygen and reduces thermal shock to the shell. Below 60 °C the scavenger cannot keep up and the boiler is thermally cycled at every feed. |

### 6.3 Boiler water limits and why each matters

Sampled from the bottom blowdown line through the sample cooler after purging.

| Parameter | Limit | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Total dissolved solids | ≤ 2,500 mg/L, target 1,800–2,200 | Above about 3,000 mg/L at this steam release rate the water surface becomes unstable and boiler water is carried over into the steam, into the presses and onto garments. |
| Conductivity at 25 °C | ≤ 4,300 µS/cm | The practical daily measurement. TDS is inferred from it using a factor of 0.58 for this water. |
| pH at 25 °C | 10.5–11.8 | Below 10.5 the protective magnetite film does not form and the steel corrodes. Above 11.8 caustic attack can occur wherever boiler water concentrates — under a deposit or at a crevice. |
| Total alkalinity as CaCO₃ | 350–700 mg/L | Buffers the pH. Too little and the pH swings; too much and the water foams. |
| Caustic (OH) alkalinity as CaCO₃ | 200–400 mg/L | The fraction that actually maintains the protective film. |
| Phosphate as PO₄ | 30–60 mg/L | Precipitates any hardness that gets past the softener as a soft, non-adherent sludge that blows down instead of scale that sticks. It is the second line of defence, not a substitute for the softener. |
| Sulphite as SO₃ | 20–50 mg/L | The residual oxygen scavenger. A residual proves no free oxygen is left. Zero sulphite means oxygen is getting through. |
| Silica as SiO₂ | ≤ 120 mg/L | Limits silica scale and volatile silica carryover. |
| Total hardness | Nil detectable | Any detectable hardness means the softener has failed or been bypassed. Stop and find out why. |
| Suspended solids | ≤ 200 mg/L | Sludge loading, controlled by bottom blowdown. |
| Oil and grease | Nil detectable | See feedwater. |

### 6.4 Softener operation and regeneration

The duplex softener has two resin vessels: one in service while the other is in
standby or regenerating, so softened water is always available.

**In service.** Raw water passes down through resin in the sodium form. Calcium
and magnesium exchange onto the resin and sodium is released into the water. The
total dissolved solids are essentially unchanged — the hardness ions have simply
been replaced with sodium, which does not form scale.

**Capacity.** Each vessel holds 25 L of resin at a working capacity of 55 g
CaCO₃ per litre, giving 1,375 g per vessel. Local town water at the plant runs
at approximately 110 mg/L total hardness as CaCO₃, so theoretical throughput is
1,375,000 ÷ 110 = 12,500 L, or 12.5 m³. **The regeneration meter is set at
10.0 m³** to hold a 20% reserve, with a 7-day calendar override so a vessel that
has stood idle regenerates anyway.

**Regeneration** takes approximately 55 minutes in five stages:

1. **Backwash, 10 min** — upward flow lifts and reclassifies the bed and flushes
   out suspended solids.
2. **Brine draw, 20 min** — saturated brine is drawn through an eductor and
   passes slowly down through the bed; sodium displaces the accumulated calcium
   and magnesium.
3. **Slow rinse, 10 min** — softened water at brine flow rate displaces the last
   of the brine through the bed.
4. **Fast rinse, 10 min** — full flow rinse to clear residual brine.
5. **Brine refill, 5 min** — measured water returns to the brine tank to dissolve
   salt for the next regeneration.

**Salt.** Coarse solar or vacuum salt suitable for water softening, minimum
99.5% NaCl, low in insolubles. Do not use table salt, dishwasher salt, pool salt
or salt with anti-caking additives. Consumption is 4.0 kg per regeneration; the
90 kg brine tank therefore holds about eleven weeks at the expected frequency.
**Never let the salt level fall below the level of the water in the tank.** A
softener regenerating on water alone does nothing at all, and the first sign will
be hardness in the boiler days later.

**Daily check.** Test the softened water hardness at the sample point. The result
must be **less than 1 mg/L as CaCO₃** — in practice a clean blue endpoint on the
first drop. If it is not: check the brine tank has salt above the water level;
initiate a manual regeneration of the in-service vessel; re-test. If hardness is
still present the softener has a fault — a leaking valve, a failed eductor,
fouled resin or a broken distributor. Call the treatment supplier. Do not simply
carry on and increase the phosphate.

### 6.5 Chemical dosing

Three products are dosed. They do different jobs and none substitutes for
another.

**Oxygen scavenger — Solvex BT-40** (catalysed sodium sulphite solution). Dosed
continuously into the hotwell by a metering pump. Removes dissolved oxygen by
reacting with it to form sulphate. Nominal dose 4.5 mL per 100 L of make-up,
trimmed to hold a boiler water sulphite residual of 20–50 mg/L as SO₃. Dosing
into the hotwell rather than the boiler gives the reaction time to complete
before the water reaches the shell and protects the feed pump and feed line too.

**Alkalinity and phosphate treatment — Solvex BT-55** (blended caustic and
sodium phosphate with a sludge conditioner). Dosed continuously into the
hotwell. Holds boiler water pH at 10.5–11.8 and phosphate at 30–60 mg/L, and
keeps precipitated hardness dispersed as a mobile sludge so it can be blown
down. Nominal dose 6.0 mL per 100 L of make-up, trimmed on the phosphate and
alkalinity results.

**Condensate line treatment — Solvex BT-70** (neutralising amine blend). Dosed
into the hotwell or the steam header. Carries over with the steam, dissolves in
the condensate and neutralises the carbonic acid formed when feedwater
alkalinity breaks down in the boiler. Target condensate pH 8.2–9.0. Nominal dose
2.0 mL per 100 L of make-up. Without it, return lines, traps and press platens
corrode from the inside and return iron to the hotwell — which is why feedwater
iron is on the test list.

**CAUTION.** BT-40 and BT-55 are alkaline. Wear splash goggles, gloves and an
apron when handling or decanting. Do not mix products or decant one into
another's container. Keep drums closed, bunded and labelled. Do not dose
chemicals by hand directly into the boiler through the feed line.

**RECORD.** Every result goes on the water test record at 13.3 on the day it is

### 6.7 What happens when limits are exceeded

| Condition | What is happening | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Hardness in feedwater | Softener exhausted, bypassed or faulty | Regenerate; if unresolved call the supplier; increase blowdown until resolved |
| Hardness in boiler water | Hardness has been entering for some time | As above, plus phosphate to the top of range, increased bottom blowdown, and an internal inspection at the next opportunity |
| Boiler TDS above 2,500 mg/L | Insufficient blowdown, or high feed TDS | Increase surface blowdown; carry out a bottom blowdown; re-test |
| Boiler TDS below 1,200 mg/L | Excessive blowdown | Reduce the surface blowdown setting — every litre blown down was heated with gas |
| pH below 10.5 | Insufficient alkalinity dose, or condensate contamination | Increase BT-55; check the condensate |
| pH above 11.8 | Overdosing, or excessive concentration | Reduce BT-55; increase blowdown |
| Sulphite residual zero | Oxygen entering; scavenger exhausted or pump failed | Check the dosing pump and drum immediately — oxygen pitting starts within days |
| Phosphate residual zero | Consumed by hardness, or pump failed | Check the pump; test feedwater hardness; suspect the softener |
| Foaming or carryover — wet steam, water hammer, presses spitting | High TDS, high alkalinity, oil or suspended solids | Blow down heavily, test for oil, reduce load, investigate the condensate |
| Feedwater iron rising | Condensate line corrosion | Check condensate pH; increase BT-70 |
| Feedwater below 70 °C | Hotwell heating failed, or excess cold make-up | Restore heating; increase scavenger temporarily |

### 6.8 Scale, and what it costs in gas

Scale is a thermal insulator between the flame and the water, with a
conductivity one to two orders of magnitude below that of steel. A film of scale
forces the furnace metal temperature up to move the same heat, which wastes gas
and, if thick enough, takes the steel past the temperature at which it holds its
strength.

| Scale thickness | Additional gas consumption |
|---|---|
| 0.4 mm | approximately 2% |
| 0.8 mm | approximately 4% |
| 1.6 mm | approximately 8% |
| 3.2 mm | approximately 14% |

Applied to this installation: at 1,307 MJ/h full fire and an annual consumption
of the order of 1,100 GJ, a 1.6 mm scale film costs roughly 88 GJ of gas a year
for nothing — more than the entire annual cost of salt, chemicals and testing,
several times over, and recovered the moment the softener is put right.

Scale is cumulative and, once formed, is not removed by better water. It is
removed by acid cleaning, which is a specialist operation, takes the boiler out
of service, requires neutralisation and disposal of the spent acid to trade waste
requirements, and carries its own risk of attacking the parent metal. The
economics are entirely one-sided: keep the softener working.

| High limit lockout, manual reset | 655 kPa |
| Safety relief valve set pressure | 690 kPa |
| Transmitter fault thresholds | < 3.5 mA or > 20.5 mA (B17) |

The controller cross-checks the transmitter against the high limit switch. A
disagreement of more than 40 kPa raises B18. **Do not adjust the operating
setpoint above 620 kPa or the high limit above 655 kPa.**

### 7.3 Water level control

A single 4-tip conductivity probe assembly in a DN50 flanged pocket carries, from
the top down: pump-off tip, pump-on tip, first low water tip, second low water
tip. The probe works on the conductivity of the boiler water between the tip and
the shell, which is why boiler water TDS below about 800 mg/L can cause
intermittent probe faults (B08, B09).

| Tip | Height above furnace crown | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Pump off | 285 mm | Feed pump stops |
| Pump on | 245 mm | Feed pump starts |
| First low water | 190 mm | Burner off, alarm, auto reset (B05) |
| Second low water | 165 mm (LWL) | Burner lockout, manual reset (B06) |

The **secondary low water cut-out** is a separate, independently mounted probe
in its own pocket with its own relay and its own manual reset button. It is not
part of the level control assembly and does not share a cable with it. AS 2593
requires this independence for limited attendance operation: a single fault —
a shorted cable, a failed relay, a scaled probe — must not be able to disable
both low water devices.

### 7.4 Gauge glass

Reflex gauge glass, 300 mm visible length, with isolating cocks top and bottom
and a drain cock below. The gauge glass is the only direct, unpowered indication
of water level on the boiler and it takes precedence over every electronic
indication. **If the gauge glass and the level display disagree, believe the
gauge glass** and shut the boiler down until the disagreement is resolved.

Fit the guard before pressurising. Never look directly along the axis of a gauge
glass while blowing it down.

### 7.5 Safety relief valve

Full-lift spring-loaded valve, DN40 inlet, DN50 outlet, set at 690 kPa, certified
relieving capacity 520 kg/h at 3% overpressure — greater than the 470 kg/h
maximum evaporation, as required. Sealed by the accredited valve service
organisation at every reset. Overhaul, test and reset interval 24 months.

An easing gear is fitted. It is used only during the annual control function
test, under supervision, and only with the boiler at not less than 75% of set
pressure. Easing a valve at low pressure drags the disc across the seat and
causes it to leak afterwards.

### 7.6 Flame supervision

Self-checking ultraviolet scanner viewing the pilot and main flame through a
quartz window in the burner mounting. The scanner shutters itself once per cycle
to prove it is not stuck in the flame-present state, which is what produces B33
if the check fails. Signal strength is displayed on the controller in arbitrary
units, 0–100. Minimum acceptable running signal is 25 units; below that, B34 is
raised as a warning and the boiler continues to run. Below 12 units the
controller treats the flame as lost and shuts down on B32.

Clean the quartz window at every service. A sooted window looks exactly like a
weak flame to the controller.

### 7.7 Purge and air proving

Pre-purge is 30 seconds at full air, which delivers not less than four changes of
the 0.31 m³ furnace volume plus the flue. Post-purge is 15 seconds. The purge
cannot be shortened, bypassed or interrupted; an interruption returns the
sequence to step 1 and restarts it.

The air proving switch is a differential pressure switch set at 0.6 kPa across
the fan. It must be **open** before the fan starts (proving it is not welded
closed — B20), **closed** within 8 seconds of the fan starting (B21), and remain
closed throughout purge and run (B22).

### 7.8 Gas pressure switches and valve proving

| Device | Setting | Reset |
|---|---|---|
| Low gas pressure switch | 8 kPa falling | Manual (B25) |
| High gas pressure switch | 25 kPa rising | Manual (B26) |
| V1 proof of closure | Contact closed with valve seated | Automatic (B29) |
| V2 proof of closure | Contact closed with valve seated | Automatic (B29) |
| Valve proving system | Pressure rise or fall > 0.5 kPa in the 30 s test | Manual (B27, B28) |

The valve proving system tests the space between V1 and V2 before every start.
It closes both valves, monitors the pressure in the test volume, and locks out if
either valve is passing. It is mandatory for this appliance at 363 kW input and
must never be disabled.

### 7.9 Feedwater pump

Multistage centrifugal, 2.2 kW, delivering 1,100 L/h at 800 kPa — approximately
2.3 times the maximum evaporation rate, so the boiler can be fed at full fire
while recovering level. Protected by a motor overload (B11), a run timeout of
8 minutes of continuous running without reaching pump-off (B12), and a hotwell
low level switch (B13).

### 7.10 Proof testing of safety devices

**STATUTORY.** These tests are the basis on which the boiler is permitted to
operate with limited attendance. Every test is recorded on the weekly test record
at 13.2, with the date, the result and the initials of the person who did it. A
test that is not recorded has not been done.

| Device | Test | Frequency | Expected result |

full fire, and never leave the boiler during it.

## 8. OPERATION

### 8.1 Before any start

1. Read the log book entry from the previous shift.
2. Check the plant room: no leaks, no smell of gas, ventilation openings clear,
   nothing stored in the clearances.
3. Check the hotwell level and temperature (82–90 °C) and the chemical drum
   levels.
4. Check the softened water hardness (6.4).
5. Check the water level in the gauge glass is at or near NWL.
6. Check the safety valve and its discharge are clear and undamaged.
7. Check the boiler stop valve and feed valves are in their normal positions.
8. Confirm no outstanding lockout on the controller.

### 8.2 Cold start

A cold start is a start from ambient temperature — typically the first start of
the week, or after any shutdown of more than 24 hours.

1. Confirm the water level at NWL. If low, fill through the feed pump in manual
   until the level is correct. Do not overfill.
2. Open the vent (vacuum breaker) to atmosphere.
3. Open the electrical isolator; energise the panel.
4. Open the manual gas isolation valve.
5. Select LOW FIRE HOLD on the controller.
6. Start the boiler. Confirm the sequence at 7.1 proceeds to main flame.
7. Hold at low fire until steam issues steadily from the open vent and the
   pressure gauge begins to lift — normally 6 to 8 minutes. **This drives air
   out of the steam space; air in the steam space causes uneven heating and
   poor heat transfer at the presses.**
8. Close the vent.
9. Release from low fire hold and allow the boiler to raise pressure under
   modulation. Do not force the rate of rise.
10. Open the boiler stop valve slowly — a quarter turn, wait, then continue —
    once the boiler is within 100 kPa of header pressure. Opening it quickly
    will cause carryover and water hammer in the header.
11. Carry out the daily checks at 7.10 once at working pressure.
12. **RECORD** the start in the log book.

Expected time from cold to 620 kPa with the stop valve closed is approximately
12 minutes. A markedly longer time indicates fouling, scale, low gas pressure or
a burner out of adjustment.

### 8.3 Warm start

A start from standby, or after a shutdown of less than 24 hours where the boiler
is still above 100 kPa.

1. Confirm the water level and that the vent is closed.
2. Confirm the stop valve position.
3. Start the boiler; the controller runs the full sequence at 7.1 regardless of
   boiler temperature. There is no shortened sequence.
4. Recovery from 500 kPa to 620 kPa is approximately 90 seconds.

### 8.4 Normal running

The operator's running duties are: watch the gauge glass at least hourly;
confirm the feed pump cycles rather than running continuously; watch the
pressure cycling between 550 and 620 kPa; listen for water hammer or a change in
burner note; and complete the daily log at 13.1.

The feed pump should run for 40–90 seconds in each cycle at a typical plant
load. Continuous running with the level falling means the pump cannot keep up —
suspect a leak, a passing blowdown valve, a fouled strainer or a failing pump.
Continuous running with the level steady means a passing feed check valve is
returning water to the hotwell.

### 8.5 Pressure setpoints in service

| Setting | Value | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| Modulation band | 480–620 kPa | Technician |
| Cut-out | 620 kPa | Technician |
| Cut-in | 550 kPa | Technician |
| High limit | 655 kPa | Technician, sealed |
| Safety valve | 690 kPa | Valve service organisation, sealed |

Presses and finishing equipment at this plant are supplied through a reducing
station and do not require the boiler to be run above 620 kPa. Raising the
boiler setpoint to speed up a press wastes gas, increases carryover, narrows the
margin to the high limit and is not permitted.

### 8.6 Blowdown

There are two kinds and they do different jobs. Both must be done.

**Bottom blowdown** removes sludge and suspended solids that have settled at the
base of the water space. It is short, sharp and infrequent.

1. Carry out at low fire or with the burner off, at a water level at or slightly
   above NWL, and never during a period of high steam demand.
2. Confirm the blowdown vessel vent is clear.
3. Wear a face shield, gauntlets and an apron.
4. Open the second (sealing) valve fully first.
5. Open the first (quick-opening lever) valve fully for **4 to 6 seconds**, then
   close it sharply.
6. Close the second valve.
7. Watch the gauge glass throughout. If the level does not recover, stop and
   investigate.
8. **RECORD** the blowdown, the duration and the operator's initials at 13.4.

Frequency: **once per operating day**, at the end of the shift. Increase to
twice daily if suspended solids exceed 150 mg/L or if hardness has been detected
in the feedwater. Never exceed 6 seconds in one operation, and never blow down
two boilers into a common vessel at the same time.

**Surface (skimmer) blowdown** removes dissolved solids from the water surface,
where they concentrate, and is what actually controls boiler water TDS. It is
continuous or near-continuous, through the DN20 needle valve at the normal water
line.

Set the needle valve so that boiler water conductivity holds in the 3,100 to
3,800 µS/cm band (TDS 1,800–2,200 mg/L) under normal load. Adjust by a quarter
turn at a time and re-test the next day; the boiler takes many hours to settle
after a change. Record the dial setting whenever it is changed.

**CAUTION.** Bottom blowdown does not control TDS — it is far too short. Surface
blowdown does not remove sludge — it takes from the wrong place. Substituting
one for the other will either scale the boiler or foam it.

### 8.7 Normal shutdown

1. Reduce load; allow the boiler to fall to low fire.
2. Switch the burner off at the controller and allow the post-purge to complete.
3. Close the boiler stop valve.
4. Allow the feed pump to bring the level to NWL, then switch the pump to off.
5. Carry out the daily bottom blowdown if not already done.
6. Close the manual gas isolation valve if the boiler will be off overnight.
7. Leave the electrical supply on so the panel and any frost protection remain
   live, unless the boiler is being isolated for work.
8. **RECORD** the shutdown, the hours run and the final readings.

### 8.8 Emergency shutdown

Press the emergency stop. This closes both gas safety shut-off valves
immediately and stops the fan and the feed pump. Then close the manual gas
isolation valve, and isolate electrically if it is safe to do so. Do not reset
an emergency stop until the reason for it has been established and dealt with.
Record every emergency shutdown in the log book with the reason.

### 8.9 Extended shutdown and lay-up

A boiler standing idle with air and water in it corrodes faster than a boiler in
service. Any shutdown expected to exceed **seven days** requires a lay-up.

**Wet lay-up** — for shutdowns of one week to about three months, where the
boiler may be needed at short notice.

1. Shut down and allow to cool to below 80 °C, but do not allow it to go cold.
2. Fill the boiler completely with softened, treated water through the vent
   until it overflows, excluding all air.
3. Raise the sulphite residual to **150–200 mg/L as SO₃** and the pH to
   **11.0–11.5** by dosing directly into the fill water. These are lay-up
   concentrations, well above running limits, and they must be brought back
   within running limits by blowdown before the boiler is returned to service.
4. Close all valves and the vent. Fit a lay-up tag to the panel.
5. Test the sulphite and pH **weekly** and re-dose as required. A wet lay-up
   that is not tested is not a lay-up.
6. **DANGER.** A wet-laid-up boiler has an oxygen-depleted water space. See 2.6.

**Dry lay-up** — for shutdowns beyond about three months, or over a plant
closure.

1. Shut down, cool, drain completely through the shell drain while still warm so
   the residual heat dries the internals.
2. Open the handholes and the burner; confirm the interior is dry.
3. Place trays of silica gel or activated alumina in the water space, at a rate
   of 2 kg per 100 L of boiler volume — 8 kg for an FB-030.
4. Close and seal all openings. Tag the boiler.
5. Inspect the desiccant every three months and renew it when spent.
6. Isolate gas and electricity.

**Returning from lay-up.** Drain, flush, refill with treated softened water,
bring the chemistry within running limits, carry out a full control function
test to 7.10 before firing, and check whether an AS 3788 inspection has fallen
due during the shutdown. A boiler that has been out of service for more than
twelve months should not be returned to service without an internal inspection.

## 10. FAULT CODES

### 10.1 How to read this section

The Vantage 3 controller displays a code, a short message and a reset class.
The reset class governs what an operator may do.

**Class A — operator resettable.** A non-safety condition. The operator may
press RESET **once**. If the same code returns, it becomes Class B.

**Class B — technician only.** A lockout arising from a safety device, or a
repeat of a Class A code. **The boiler must not be reset, re-reset or run until
a licensed technician has attended, found the cause and rectified it.** Repeated
resetting of a safety lockout is the single most dangerous act an operator can
commit on a boiler: every reset re-arms a burner whose safety chain has already
told you something is wrong.

**Class C — gasfitter only.** A lockout in the gas train or the valve proving
system. Only a licensed Type B gasfitter may investigate or rectify it.

**RECORD.** Every lockout is entered in the log book with the code, the time,
what was happening at the time, and who was called. Codes B05, B06, B16, B25,
B26, B27, B30, B31, B32 and B33 are also to be reported to the person with
management or control of the plant on the day they occur.

Codes not listed below (B14, B19 and B37) are reserved and are not generated by
Rev 7 firmware. A reserved code appearing on the display indicates a controller
fault — see B38.

### 10.3 Resetting — the rule

An operator may press RESET **once** on a Class A code, having first carried out
the operator check listed against it. If the code returns, or if the operator
does not understand why it appeared, the boiler is shut down, the gas isolated
and the technician called.

An operator may **never** reset a Class B or Class C code. B06, B16, B25, B26,
B27, B28, B29 and B33 in particular are lockouts on devices that exist solely to
prevent an explosion or a shell failure. A boiler that has locked out on one of
these has already had its first line of protection used up.

### 10.4 Troubleshooting — the eight most common

**B05 — low water, first.** By far the most frequent code on this boiler, and
almost always a feedwater problem rather than a boiler problem. Work through it
in this order: is there water in the hotwell; is the make-up supply on; is the
softener stuck in regeneration and starving the hotwell; is the feed pump
running when it is called; is the feed line stop valve open; is the strainer
blocked; is the bottom blowdown valve passing, which shows as a hot blowdown
line between blowdowns; is there a visible leak. A single B05 at the start of a
heavy Monday, with a hotwell that had not been refilled over the weekend, is
explicable and resettable once. A B05 that recurs is not — the boiler has six
minutes of water at full fire and something is taking it away.

**B30 — ignition failure.** Usually the ignition electrode. The gap widens as
the electrode erodes, and the porcelain cracks from thermal cycling and tracks
to earth so the spark never reaches the gap. Also check the pilot gas pressure
at 2.5 kPa and that the pilot solenoid is being energised. A boiler that lights
on the second or third attempt every morning is telling you the electrode is due
— replace it (FB-1051) rather than waiting for the lockout.

**B32 — flame failure in run.** Distinguish between a flame that was genuinely
lost and a flame the scanner stopped seeing. If the signal strength has been
logged as drifting down and a B34 has been raised in the preceding weeks, the
quartz window is sooted or the scanner is failing. If the signal was strong and
then vanished, look at the gas supply — a dip when another large appliance
starts — or at flame stability at low fire. Recurrent B32 at low fire generally
means the low fire air setting is too high and the flame is being blown off the
head; that is a combustion adjustment and a gasfitter's work.

**B21 — air proving failure.** Check the simple things in order: is the fan
turning; is the inlet filter or the fan inlet blocked with lint, which in a dry
cleaning plant it very often is; is the sensing line to the air switch
connected, unblocked and not full of water; are the ventilation openings clear;
has anything been stacked against the louvre. Then check the flue for
obstruction. A B21 that appears only when a particular extraction fan runs is a
room pressure problem, not a boiler fault, and it will not be fixed at the
boiler.

**B25 — low gas pressure.** The first check is the manual isolation valve, which
is more often left part-closed after work than anyone admits. Then the gas
filter: a filter blocked with pipe scale drops enough pressure at full fire to
trip the switch while reading normally at low fire, which is why B25 typically
appears at the moment the burner tries to modulate up. Then the appliance
regulator. Do not adjust any of this. It is Class C.

**B27 — valve proving leak.** A safety shut-off valve is passing gas. It is
usually a seat contaminated with a fragment of pipe scale or rust, which is why
it appears in the months after gas work upstream. It may also be a failed valve.
Either way, gas is leaking past a valve that is supposed to be gas-tight, into a
furnace that may be unpurged. There is no operator action, no reset, and no
circumstance in which the boiler may be run.

**B08 and B09 — level probe faults.** These two are opposite symptoms of the
same maintenance failure. B08, stuck wet, is a tip bridged by scale or sludge,
or a cable that has been damaged and is shorting: the controller believes there
is water where there is none, which is the dangerous direction. B09, stuck dry,
is an open circuit, a broken tip, or — commonly — boiler water conductivity that
has fallen too low for the probe to conduct, which happens after over-blowdown
or after a refill. Check the conductivity first: below about 800 µS/cm the probe
is unreliable. If conductivity is normal, the probe must be withdrawn, cleaned
and insulation-tested. **Whenever the display and the gauge glass disagree, the
gauge glass is right.**

**B35 — flue gas high temperature.** The flue gas temperature at full fire on a
clean boiler with clean water is 240 °C. It rises as the fireside fouls with
soot or the waterside fouls with scale, because heat that is no longer going
into the water goes up the flue instead. A rise of 20 K above the commissioned
figure is worth investigating; 320 °C is a lockout. Check the combustion figures
first — a burner running rich soots the fireside quickly. If combustion is
correct and the temperature is still climbing, the boiler is scaling, and the
answer is at Section 6, not at the burner.

### 10.5 Further troubleshooting notes

**B10 — high water.** Water above the pump-off tip is not harmless. The steam
space on an FB-030 is only 165 litres, and flooding it puts water into the dry
pipe and out into the header. The presses will spit, the traps will flood and a
water hammer in the header can break a fitting. The usual cause is a feed check
valve that is not seating, allowing the header pressure to push water back and
forth, or a pump-off tip bridged with sludge. If the level continues to rise with
the pump stopped, isolate the feed line manually and call the technician.

**B12 and B13 — the feed system.** Treat these two together, because the boiler
does not care which one is the root cause: both end in a boiler that cannot be
fed. B13 says there is no water in the hotwell to pump. The order of enquiry is
the town supply, the pressure reducing valve, the softener (a softener stuck in
regeneration will starve the hotwell for the better part of an hour), the break
tank, and the float valve. B12 says the pump ran for eight minutes without
recovering level. That is either a pump that is not delivering — air-bound,
worn, running backwards after electrical work, or drawing through a blocked
strainer — or water leaving the boiler as fast as it goes in. Feel the blowdown
line: a bottom blowdown valve that is not seating will be hot between blowdowns,
and it is the single most commonly missed cause of a boiler that will not hold
level.

**B16 — high limit lockout.** The high limit exists because the operating control
failed. Something allowed the boiler to fire past 620 kPa. Before anything else,
confirm the safety valve has not lifted; if it has, that is a separate record and
a separate inspection. Then look for the reason the burner did not shut down at
cut-out: a failed pressure transmitter, a blocked siphon (which makes the
transmitter read low and the burner fire on), a controller output relay welded
closed, or a gas valve passing. The most common of these on this boiler is the
blocked siphon — it fills with scale from carryover, and it is a symptom of a
water treatment problem rather than a control problem. **B16 is never resettable
by an operator, and the boiler is not to be run again until the reason the
operating control failed has been identified.**

**B18 — sensor disagreement.** The controller compares the transmitter reading
against the state of the high limit switch and against the mechanical gauge
reading entered at each service. A disagreement of more than 40 kPa means one of
the two pressure measurements is wrong, and there is no way to tell from the
panel which one. Record both the display and the mechanical gauge in the log book
before calling the technician — that pair of numbers is the first thing they will
ask for and it usually identifies the faulty device without any test gear.

**B36 — burner fan overload.** In a dry cleaning plant this is nearly always
lint. The fan draws air from the plant room; the plant room is full of airborne
fibre from the dryers and the finishing equipment; the fibre collects on the
impeller and in the inlet cone, unbalances the impeller and loads the motor. The
same fibre is what eventually causes B21. Cleaning the fan inlet at every
quarterly service, and keeping the boiler room door closed, prevents both.

**B02 and B38 — controller faults.** Neither is repairable on site and neither
may be worked around. B38 in particular means the controller has failed its own
internal safety check: the device responsible for closing the gas valves can no
longer prove that it is capable of closing them. Isolate gas and electricity and
leave the boiler shut down until the controller is replaced.

**Intermittent faults.** A fault that clears itself is still a fault and is still
recorded. Note the code, the time, the firing rate at the time, the steam
pressure, the water level and what else in the plant was running or starting. An
intermittent B22, for example, is meaningless on its own but obvious once three
entries in the log book all coincide with the dry cleaning machine's extraction
fan starting. The log book is the diagnostic instrument for intermittent faults;
memory is not.

  attachment to a pressure part, without written approval.
