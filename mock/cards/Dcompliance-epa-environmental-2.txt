Second part of the Main Street Dry Cleaners environmental compliance manual, covering solvent storage and bunding, decanting, spill kits and waste.
The solvent store is locked and mechanically ventilated, keys with Karen Whitfield and Peter Nguyen only. It holds up to 2 x 200 L drums each of perc and hydrocarbon solvent in separate bunded bays, plus a waste bay.
Bund rule: the greater of 110% of the largest single container and 25% of the aggregate stored. For 2 x 200 L drums that is 220 L required. The perc bund is 1,800 x 1,200 x 160 mm, so 345.6 L available, a margin of 125.6 L. The hydrocarbon bay is identical; the waste bay covers a 205 L drum, requiring 225.5 L.
Bunds have no drain, valve or outlet. Liquid found in a bund is removed within 24 hours into a labelled drum as reportable priority waste, never mopped to a floor waste.
Two spill kits, checked monthly: a 240 L bin in the store and a 60 L kit at the machine.
Maximum held: 400 L perc, 400 L hydrocarbon, about 60 L of process chemistry. The site reaches neither the placarding nor the manifest quantity for any class, so neither is required; a chemical register and door placard are kept anyway.
Perc transfer is closed system only, by drum pump and closed coupling over the bund, with low-level store extraction running. Open pouring, siphoning and transfer by a lone worker are prohibited. Gloves laminate or Viton, never nitrile.
Waste: perc still bottoms, spent filters, spent carbon, separator water and perc-contaminated rags are Category B reportable priority waste, code N220, halogenated organic solvents and residues. Hydrocarbon still residues are code N205, Category C.
Maximum waste held: 2 x 205 L drums of Category B plus a 60 L separator water drum, never more than 12 months from the date first filled.
