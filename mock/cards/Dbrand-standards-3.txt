Brand and Marketing Standards, third of three parts — the claim letter, the at-risk form, digital, reviews and the counter script.
MSDC-BR-01, version 3.0, issued 1 February 2026.
Claim letters go on letterhead and are never handed across the counter. They open with what happened and that it happened here, state the offer in figures, and are signed in full. Never "as per our terms", "we deny liability" or the tagline. Anything to a customer about a claim is written by Karen Whitfield or Sharon Delaney, nobody else.
At-risk authorisation: A5 Bone card, two-part carbonless, number in IBM Plex Mono, body text 10 pt minimum, the risk handwritten in the staff member's own words, never ticked from a list or called a waiver. Filed 7 years.
Website: home, services, wedding gowns, pickup and delivery, prices, about, contact. No blog, chat or pop-up. Checked in February.
Google Business Profile: Nadia updates; Karen approves description, category and services. Hours entered seven days ahead; questions answered in one business day.
Every review gets a response. Positive: two sentences and a name. Negative: written by Karen or Sharon only, never the same day it lands, under 80 words — thank them, acknowledge the specific thing, never argue or correct them publicly, never disclose anything about their garment, and move it offline with a direct number.
Social: two posts a month, Nadia drafts and Karen approves. Never a customer's garment without written permission, nothing under claim.
Counter script: inspect, ask, tell, promise — the promise a date and a time. When something has gone wrong: "This happened here and I'm sorry. I'd like to sort it out properly rather than quickly." Take details, have Karen ring tomorrow. Never say waiver, disclaimer, liability or at your own risk.
