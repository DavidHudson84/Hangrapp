SOP 02 Handling a Complaint, second of three parts — the six fault questions, the four outcomes, the ACL and the Fair Claims Guide.
MSDC-SOP-02, version 3, effective 1 March 2026.
The six questions: was the care label followed; was the method gentler than the label allowed; is there an MSDC-F02, signed before acceptance, naming this failure mode; is it a known fabric or manufacturing fault (name the DLI TABS bulletin, and AS/NZS 1957 where the label ignores a trim); is it wear; is it something we did?
Four outcomes: defend, deny, settle, goodwill. If between two, Karen decides — the tie does not go to the business.
ACL section 60 guarantees due care and skill — a competent job, not a perfect outcome — and cannot be excluded by a sign, the docket or a signed F02.
The International Fair Claims Guide: Table I gives life expectancy, Table II a percentage of replacement cost by age band and condition at intake. Settlement is depreciated value and the working is always shown.
Table I life expectancy: wool or wool blend suit 3 years; fancy dress 2; plain everyday dress 3; wool jumper or cardigan 4; wool overcoat 5; wedding gown after the wedding 3; leather or suede jacket 4; lined curtains and drapes 5.
Worked example: wool jumper, replacement $640, 14 months old so the 13 to 25 month band, excellent condition, Table II 70% — $448.00, plus the $24.00 cleaning charge waived.
A gown cleaned after the wedding is capped at 50% of replacement cost whatever Table II gives: a $3,200 gown that would attract 70% ($2,240) settles at $1,600.00. Before the wedding, no cap.
The cleaning charge is always waived when we settle and is not part of the figure. On payment of the full adjusted value the garment becomes ours, though the customer may keep it with the figure adjusted by agreement.
