THE GARMENT

AllSaints black lambskin biker jacket, asymmetric front zip, quilted polyester shoulder panels, viscose lining. Purchased from AllSaints Melbourne in 2021; approximately 5 years old. Value stated by the customer: $1,100. Service requested by the customer: full clean and removal of the mark on the lower back.

THE CARE LABEL

Present and legible, stitched into the lining seam. It carries no care symbol. The tag reads, in full: "By leather specialist only — colour change may occur."

CONDITION AT INTAKE

The following were present when the jacket was handed over the counter and were photographed before it was accepted. Nine photographs were taken, including the sleeve colour difference, both cuffs, the collar and the back mark with a scale.

1. The right sleeve is approximately two shades lighter than the body and the left sleeve. This is sun bleaching, consistent with the customer's account of driving with that arm at the window.
2. The applied surface finish is worn through to the bare skin at both cuffs, on the outer face, over an area of roughly 60 mm on each.
3. The applied surface finish is worn through along the full length of the collar fold.
4. Colour is uneven across the front and back panels generally, independent of the sleeve difference.
5. A stiffened area approximately 150 mm across sits on the lower back, where a liquid of unknown composition has dried into the skin. The hand of the leather in that area is noticeably harder than the surrounding panels.
6. The quilted polyester shoulder panels are a different substrate to the leather and have worn and soiled differently.
7. General wear consistent with five years of use: creasing at the elbows, softening at the front edges, minor scuffing at the hem.

WHAT WE PROPOSE TO DO

The jacket will not be cleaned in our own plant. It will be sent to our specialist leather cleaner, who will clean it in a leather-specific process and then re-finish it. The whole jacket will be re-finished, not spot-finished. Spot re-finishing the cuffs, collar and back would leave visible edges where new finish meets old, and on a jacket whose colour is already uneven it would look worse than what you have now. Re-finishing the whole garment is the only way to bring the panels back to one colour.

THE RISKS

1. The colour will change. Re-finishing lays down new colour over the whole jacket and it may not return to the original shade. The finished jacket may read lighter, darker or slightly different in tone to the black you brought in.
2. Further loss of finish at the cuffs and along the collar fold is likely. Cleaning removes soil and it also removes finish that is already failing. Those areas may look worse before they are re-finished, and the new finish may not hold as well there as it does elsewhere.
3. The mark on the lower back may not come out at all. It is set, it has stiffened the skin, and its composition is unknown. The stiffening may remain even if the colour of the mark is covered by the re-finish.
4. The quilted shoulder panels may not take the re-finish evenly. They are a textile, not leather, and they will absorb finish differently to the skin around them.
5. Turnaround is 3 to 4 weeks, not our usual 5 days, because the work is done off site.

THE DLI POSITION

DLI TABS — Leather, Suede, Velvet & Napped Fabrics records that these risks are inherent to leather cleaning rather than the result of any error by the cleaner. Leather is a natural skin, not a woven fabric. Its colour is an applied surface finish over a substrate that varies in density, oil content and absorbency across a single hide, and cleaning necessarily removes some of the natural oils and some of that finish along with the soil. The TAB records that colour change, further loss of finish on already worn areas, and the permanence of set stains are expected outcomes on a worn garment, and that a garment already showing uneven colour and worn finish before cleaning carries a materially higher risk of an uneven result. That description fits this jacket.
