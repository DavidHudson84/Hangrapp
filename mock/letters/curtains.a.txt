We hold the line on liability, but we do not leave her with short curtains.

The measurements do the work. 2,650 mm at intake, 2,610 mm hung, so 40 mm on a 2,650 mm drop is 1.5 per cent. Relaxation shrinkage of up to 3 per cent is accepted across the trade as normal in drapery, so she is at half the allowance. Every fact points the same way: a cotton and linen union face over a cotton sateen lining, two natural fibres with different shrinkage behaviour, no pre-shrink treatment from the maker, and seven years hanging under its own weight without ever being cleaned. Curtains stretch on the rail and hold the stretch. The first clean releases it. That is the fabric's history, not our machine, and programme 9 is the mildest cycle we run on a lined curtain — 180 rpm, hung to relax straight out of the drum.

DLI TABS — Stretch, Shrinkage & Distortion states it as an inherent fabric characteristic that is neither caused nor preventable by the cleaning process. Note too that there is no care label on these at all, so there was no manufacturer's instruction as to process or shrinkage allowance and we worked to our own judgement at the mildest setting available.

The signed drapery advice from 30 March is what makes this a short conversation rather than a long one. It says in terms that up to 3 per cent relaxation is normal and not a fault. That is why we measure at the counter.

So: deny liability, decline the $2,400, and then fix her problem. There is turn-up in those hems and 40 mm is well inside it. Anh lets all four pairs down and re-presses at our cost, no charge, and Dylan can collect if she would rather not carry them in.

I'd put the denial and the free alteration in one letter to her — want me to draft it?
