This is not a claim, and I would be careful not to let it turn into one by treating it like one.

Nothing is damaged. Down is loose cluster, not batting, and it moves inside the baffle. Washing wets the clusters, they collapse together, and as the jacket dries they settle to the bottom of each channel and clump, which thins the top of every baffle and gives you exactly the cold spots she is describing. The shell, the baffle stitching, the lining and the down are all sound. It is a drying-stage outcome, and it reverses.

DLI TABS — Household Textiles, covering drapes, comforters, down and linens, records clumping after cleaning as expected rather than exceptional, and recovered by extended low-heat tumbling that separates the clusters and returns the loft. Fill power is not lost. The down just needs to be beaten apart again.

Where we did fall short is the drying, and it is fair to say so without turning it into fault. One low-heat tumble with dryer balls is our standard and it suits most jackets. A hip-length hooded jacket with horizontal baffles and 700 fill duck down is a lot of down in deep channels, and one cycle has not done it. Two or three will, with the clumps hand-broken between cycles.

So: no settlement figure, no admission, and do not put a number on the jacket — the moment you value it you have conceded it is a claim. Offer the extended re-loft at no charge, put a $40 credit on her account for having to come back twice, and make it easy — Sharon expecting her at the counter, or Dylan collects.

Keep it short and warm. She has gone away annoyed and the letter's job is to get the jacket back in the door.
