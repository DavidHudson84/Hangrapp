Take it, but only on a signed authorisation, and the authorisation has to be signed before it leaves your counter — not posted afterwards.

The reasoning is that everything wrong with this jacket now will be worse or different after cleaning, and none of it will be our doing. Leather is a skin, not a woven fabric. Its colour is an applied surface finish over a substrate that varies in density and oil content across a single hide, and cleaning necessarily takes some of that finish and some of the natural oils along with the soil. Where the finish is already worn through — both cuffs, the collar fold — it will go further. DLI TABS — Leather, Suede, Velvet & Napped Fabrics records colour change, further finish loss on worn areas and the permanence of set stains as inherent outcomes rather than cleaner error, and it flags a garment that is already uneven before cleaning as materially higher risk. This one is halfway there before we start.

Method: send it out to the specialist, and re-finish the whole jacket rather than spot-finish it. Spot re-finishing the cuffs and collar on a jacket whose sleeve is already two shades out will show every edge. Tell him 3 to 4 weeks, not our usual 5 days.

The risks he has to sign to are: the colour will change and may not come back to the original black; further finish loss at the cuffs and collar is likely; the back mark may not lift at all and the stiffening may stay even if the colour is covered; and the quilted shoulder panels are a textile, not leather, so they may not take the re-finish evenly.
