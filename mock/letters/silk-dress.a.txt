We pay this one. There is no defensible version and it is better to say so quickly than slowly.

The care label carried a specific instruction — remove belt before cleaning — and our own intake procedure says belts, sashes, scarves and detachable trims come off at the counter and get bagged separately. Two independent instructions, both missed. That is preparation, not process: programme 5 on the Union was the right cycle for a silk crepe de chine and the dress would have come out perfectly if the belt had been in a separate bag.

A separate dye lot on the belt is the aggravating fact. Emerald acid dyes on silk are among the worst for migration, and lot-to-lot variation in fixation is exactly the case where one piece bleeds onto another in solvent. DLI TABS — Dye Bleed, Migration & Colour Run covers it, and it is also why Karen was right to stop Peter. Once a bled dye has set into a silk ground, further reduction takes the ground colour before it takes the stain, and three patches become a ruined dress.

On the number, the Fair Claims Guide classes it as a fancy dress with a two year life. At eight months it sits in the 7 to 13 month band, condition excellent — worn twice, first clean. That band adjusts to 70 per cent, so on $680 the offer is $476.00, and waive the $28.00 charge.

Do not hedge the cause when you write to her. She knows what happened, we know what happened, and a customer who is told plainly that we made an error usually takes the depreciated figure without a fight. Offer to return the dress too — the marks sit where a jacket would cover them.

I'd put that in a settlement letter to Priya, cause stated plainly and the working shown — want me to draft it?
