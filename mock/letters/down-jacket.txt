Thank you for bringing the jacket back in on Saturday and for giving us the chance to look at it rather than just leaving it in the cupboard. Emma and I have both had a good look and I want to tell you what is going on, because it is better news than it looks.

The down has not been damaged. What has happened is that it has migrated. Down is loose clusters, not batting, and it moves freely inside each baffle. Washing wets those clusters, they collapse against each other, and as the jacket dries they clump and settle to the bottom of each channel. That leaves the top of every baffle thin, which is why the jacket feels flat and lumpy and why you are getting cold spots along the shoulders and the upper chest. The shell, the baffle stitching, the lining and the down itself are all sound. Nothing in your jacket is broken.

It is also a completely normal outcome and a recoverable one. DLI TABS — Household Textiles, which covers drapes, comforters, down and linens, records that clumping of down after cleaning is expected rather than exceptional, and that it is recovered by extended low-heat tumbling which separates the clusters and returns the loft. The down does not lose its fill power; it just needs to be beaten apart again.

Where we fell short is on the drying. Our standard is one low-heat tumble with dryer balls after the wash, and for most jackets that is enough. Yours is a hip-length hooded jacket with horizontal baffles and 700 fill power duck down, which is a lot of down in deep channels, and one cycle has not done the job. It needs two or three.

So here is what we would like to do. Bring the jacket back in and we will run a full extended re-loft at no charge — usually two to three cycles on low heat with dryer balls, checking and hand-breaking the clumps between cycles, which takes most of a day.
