Dear Mrs Kastellorizos,

Thank you for your call of 9 April and for letting Karen come out to the house on 14 April to look at the curtains hung. We have now gone through the docket, the intake measurements and the photographs, and I am writing to set out where we stand.

The four pairs of lined pinch-pleat curtains came in on 30 March, were cleaned on 2 April and were collected on 4 April, under docket B-22886. They were measured at the counter at intake and the measurement written on the docket was 2,650 mm from the top of the pleat to the hem. Measured again at your house on 14 April, the same pairs come to 2,610 mm. The difference is 40 mm, which on a 2,650 mm drop is 1.5 per cent.

That figure is the answer to the question. Relaxation shrinkage of up to 3 per cent is normal in curtains of this construction and is accepted throughout the trade as a fabric characteristic rather than a cleaning fault. Yours are a cotton and linen union face with a cotton sateen lining, made to measure in 2019, with no pre-shrink treatment applied by the maker — your own invoice confirms that — and they had hung for seven years without ever being cleaned. Over that time a hung curtain stretches under its own weight and holds the stretch. The first clean releases it, and the fabric returns towards the length it was woven at. Two natural fibres of different shrinkage behaviour in the face cloth, over a cotton lining, make that movement certain rather than merely possible. At 1.5 per cent, yours have moved half of what the trade allows.

DLI TABS — Stretch, Shrinkage & Distortion sets out the same position: relaxation shrinkage in drapery is an inherent characteristic of the fabric and its history on the rail, it is not caused or prevented by the cleaning process, and it is not a defect in workmanship. Our drapery programme is the mildest cycle we run on a lined curtain — extended bath, reduced mechanical action, 180 rpm extract, dried at 45 degrees C — and they were hung to relax straight out of the machine rather than folded.

This was also put to you in writing before the work began. The drapery advice you signed on 30 March states in terms that relaxation shrinkage of up to 3 per cent is normal and is not a fault, and it covers the risk to sun-weakened fabric as well. That was not a formality; it is there because this happens, and it is why we measure at the counter. The sun degradation along the leading edges of the two north-facing pairs was recorded and photographed at intake, and it is unchanged.

I should also note that the curtains carry no care label of any kind — none was supplied by the maker. Without a care label there is no manufacturer's instruction as to process, temperature or shrinkage allowance, and we handled them on our own judgement, at the mildest setting available to us, having told you in writing what the risk was.

For those reasons we do not accept liability for the change in length, and we are not able to meet the replacement quotation of $2,400.
