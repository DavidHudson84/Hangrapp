This one is ours, at least in part, and I would settle it rather than argue it.

The mechanism is straightforward. Hair product, skin oil and perspiration concentrate on the collar and the shoulder tops and weaken the dye in situ over time. The next perc cycle lifts the weakened dye along with the soil, and you get local colour loss with a hard edge at the collar seam. The trousers being untouched confirms the machine was behaving — it is the soil pattern, not the cycle. DLI TABS — Colour Fading & Discoloration sets out that mechanism and the correct handling, which is to identify the affected areas at intake and pre-spot or divert.

That is where we are exposed. Our own SOP says a collar showing product build-up goes to hydrocarbon or gets pre-spotted. Neither happened, nothing was noted at intake, and there are no intake photographs. We could talk about contributory factors all day but a Consumer Affairs officer would read our procedure, see we did not follow it, and stop there.

On the number, the Fair Claims Guide gives a wool suit a three year life. His is 36 months old, which is the 28 to 37 month band, condition average — sound and well kept but at the end of the expected life. That band adjusts to 20 per cent, so on $1,250 the offer is $250.00, and I would waive the $32.00 cleaning charge.

He will think $250 is low against a $1,250 suit, so the working has to be visible and the tone has to be straight. Do not hedge it, do not lead with sympathy, and do not tell him the collar was already weak — say we should have caught it and did not. Offer to return the suit if he would rather keep the trousers.

I'd set all of that out in a settlement letter to him — want me to draft it?
