The dye transfer on your emerald silk dress is our fault. We left the belt threaded through the loops and put it through the machine with the dress, and there is no version of this where that is anything other than our error.

The dress was dropped at our Kangaroo Flat store on 13 July and cleaned at Bendigo on 14 July under docket K-08812. The care label carries a supplementary line reading "Remove belt before cleaning". Our own intake procedure says the same thing in broader terms — belts, sashes, scarves and detachable trims come off at the counter and are bagged separately. The belt is the same silk as the dress but was dyed in a separate lot, which is why it has always been a deeper green, and a separate lot of an emerald acid dye on silk is exactly the case where dye will migrate in solvent. It did. You now have three darker patches with soft edges, at both hips and across the small of the back, between about 60 mm and 110 mm across.

Peter worked on it on the spotting board on 15 July and lifted perhaps a third of it. Karen then stopped him, because the next step risked pulling the ground colour out of the dress around the marks and turning three patches into a much larger problem. DLI TABS — Dye Bleed, Migration & Colour Run is clear that once a bled dye has set into a silk ground, aggressive reduction usually costs more colour than it recovers. So the marks that are there now are the marks that will stay.

On the money. Textile claims are settled on the International Fair Claims Guide for Consumer Textile Products, which is the standard the industry, insurers and Consumer Affairs work from, and it settles on depreciated value rather than the price of a new one. The Guide classes this as a fancy dress with a normal life expectancy of two years. Yours is eight months old, which falls in the 7 to 13 month band, and its condition is excellent — worn twice, never cleaned before, and sound in every respect apart from what we did to it. That band at excellent condition adjusts to 70 per cent of replacement cost. On a replacement cost of $680, that is $476.00.

That is depreciated value, not a new dress, and I would rather be straight about it than have you find out from someone else. It is how every textile claim in the country is worked out, and it is the number we would be held to by our own insurer.
