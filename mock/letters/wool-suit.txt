The colour loss across the collar and shoulders of your jacket is our responsibility and we accept it. You brought the jacket back on 28 May and you were right about what you were looking at. I am not going to put this on the garment.

What happened is this. The collar and the tops of the shoulders are where hair product, skin oil and perspiration concentrate on a jacket, and over three years and four cleans they build up in the fibre. Those residues weaken the dye in that area before we ever touch it. When the garment then goes through a standard perchloroethylene cycle, the solvent lifts the weakened dye along with the soil, and you get exactly what you have: the collar and shoulders two shades lighter than the body, with a defined tide line where the collar meets the jacket. The trousers went through the same load and are unchanged, which tells you the machine was doing what it should. Our own procedure says that a garment showing that kind of collar build-up goes to hydrocarbon or is pre-spotted before it goes anywhere. Neither was done, and the build-up was not recorded at intake. That is the failure, and it is ours.

For what it is worth technically, DLI TABS — Colour Fading & Discoloration sets out the same mechanism: dye weakened by body soils and grooming products fails locally in the next clean, which is why the correct handling is to identify the affected areas at intake and treat them first. We did not.

On the money. Textile claims in Australia are settled on the International Fair Claims Guide for Consumer Textile Products, which is the standard the trade, insurers and Consumer Affairs all work from. It works on depreciated value rather than replacement cost, because a garment that has been worn and cleaned for three years is not worth what a new one costs. The Guide gives a wool or wool blend suit a normal life expectancy of three years. Yours is 36 months old, which puts it in the 28 to 37 month band, and its condition on the Guide's scale is average — sound and well kept, but three years into a three year life. That band at average condition adjusts to 20 per cent of replacement cost. On a replacement cost of $1,250 for the two-piece suit, that is $250.00.

I know that figure will look low against what you paid, and I would rather say so plainly than dress it up. It is not a valuation of what the suit means to you or a comment on how well you have looked after it. It is what the Guide says the remaining life of a three year old suit is worth, and it is the same calculation we would be held to if this went to Consumer Affairs or to our insurer.

Our offer is $250.00, and we are waiving the cleaning charge of $32.00 for the suit, so nothing is payable to us. If you have already paid it, we will refund it with the settlement.

On payment of the full adjusted value the jacket and trousers become our property, which is the ordinary position under the Guide.
