The expensive part of the job

The cleaning is the easy bit.

What costs a dry cleaner money is everything around it — the claim you paid because you couldn't prove otherwise, the warning letter nobody wanted to write, the classification you guessed at.

01

Disputes you shouldn't have paid

Most disputes aren't lost on the cleaning. They're lost because nobody wrote down what the garment looked like before it went in — and by the time it's argued, it's your word against a receipt.

02

Paperwork nobody has time for

Award classifications, casual conversion, a first written warning that has to be worded properly. The jobs that get put off until they turn into a bigger problem than the one you started with.

03

A number you've never seen

Almost no operator can tell you what claims actually cost them last year, which garments and brands keep failing, or how much they didn't pay because they held the position.

What it does

02 — Defend a claim properly

It won't let you skip the facts.

Your counter staff log the incident the moment it happens. Before Hangr will draft a thing, it asks for the 21 facts that actually decide liability — the exact care-label wording, the method and solvent used, whether the label was legible, who inspected at intake, what the customer says happened.

Then it works out where fault sits, and depreciates on the International Fair Claims Guide rather than on an argument in the shop.

Worth being straight about

What Hangr isn't.

It isn't a POS or an order system. It doesn't take orders, print dockets, rack garments or run your routes. Keep whatever you use for that — Hangr sits alongside it.

It isn't the last word. It gives very good general guidance and it can still be wrong about your specific situation. On anything that really matters — a big claim, a dispute heading somewhere formal — it's a starting point for the owner, not a decision. It's general information for Australian dry cleaning operators, not legal, tax or financial advice.

It's early. Hangr is in pilot with a small number of operators while it's shaped around real shops. That's exactly why we're being choosy about who comes on next — and why the people who do get a say in what gets built.

Questions

The ones people actually ask.

Do I have to change my POS or my systems?

No. Hangr doesn't touch your point of sale, your dockets or your bookkeeping. It's a separate thing you open when you need an answer or a letter. Nothing to integrate, nothing to migrate.

What happens after I request a trial?

David emails you to find a time, and you do a Zoom call together — about 45 minutes. On the call your business gets set up properly: your sites, services, equipment and solvents, your price list, and your logins for whoever needs one. You come off the call using it rather than with a login and a help page. It's free, there's nothing to buy at the end of it, and you're not signed up to anything by doing it.

Why can't I just sign myself up?

Because Hangr is only as good as what it knows about your shop, and a form can't get that out of you. Half an hour on a call loading your price list and your documents is the difference between answers that are about your business and answers that are about dry cleaning in general. We're also taking on a small number of operators at a time while it's shaped around real shops, so it's a conversation rather than a signup button on purpose.

How long does it take to set up?

The setup itself is about twenty minutes — your business details, sites, services, equipment and solvents — and we do it with you on the onboarding call rather than leaving you to it. Upload your price list and it can start quoting off it straight away. The document library (lease, insurance, manuals, SOPs) is the part you add over the following weeks, and every document you put in makes the answers better.

Can my counter staff use it without seeing everything?

Yes, and that's the point of the roles. A staff login sees chat, the price list and their own problem reports and letters. No claims values, no staff records, no HR, and the lease and insurance policy are invisible to them in the document library. It isn't just hidden on screen — the data is never given to the AI for that login, so it can't be asked for sideways.

Is my data safe, and who can see it?

Your data is stored in Australia and is walled off from every other business on the system. It isn't sold and isn't shared for marketing. Questions are sent to Anthropic in the United States to be answered, and under their commercial API terms your inputs and the answers aren't used to train their models. The full detail, including how to export or delete everything, is in the privacy policy.

How accurate is it?

On things it can read — your price list, your lease, your equipment manuals — it quotes them back and tells you where the answer came from. On general industry questions it's drawing on the textile bulletins, the Award and the Consumer Law, and it's good. But it's an assistant, not a solicitor, and on anything that matters it says so itself.

What does it cost?

Nothing during the pilot. We're putting it in front of a small number of operators first, and pricing gets settled with the people using it rather than decided at them. Ask for a trial and we'll have that conversation properly.

What's coming next?

Bill Compare — tracking your electricity, gas and water against your contract end dates so you know when you're being quietly repriced — is built and on the pilot roadmap, not switched on yet. Beyond that, the roadmap is largely set by what pilot operators ask for.
